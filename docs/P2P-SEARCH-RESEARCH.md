## 1. Executive summary

Search on Pear is **two engines that must never be conflated**: (a) **discovery** — turning human intent/keywords into the right opaque `hyper://` 32-byte keys (a naming/directory problem, Zooko's triangle), and (b) **full-text search** — classic ranked retrieval over P2P-published page bodies. Discovery is **shippable now** on primitives we already own. Full-text is the **research-grade, deferred half** where Sybil-resistance, ranking-without-authority, query privacy, and freshness all bite at once.

The four proposals span the decentralization axis from "rebuild YaCy properly" (TermShard, score 4) through two relay/curation-anchored federations (HiveSearch 5.5, Trustweave 4.5) to a social-first personal index (Constellation 6, the highest score). No single proposal survives its review intact. **Three findings are convergent and decisive:**

1. **Do not build a global term-sharded DHT inverted index as the v1 ranking authority.** TermShard's own declared killer risk — cross-shard multi-keyword AND at interactive latency over Zipf-distributed posting lists on independent volunteer hosts — is unproven and is exactly what killed YaCy. The review found it does not hold on the evidence.
2. **Do not put a per-document corpus in a schema-sheets / Autobase room.** Verified directly: `SchemaSheets.list()` (sheets-bundle.cjs:46295) materializes *every* row of a schema with `.toArray()` then JMESPath-scans in JS; there is no working term index and the `MAX_SHEETS_ROWS` cap is *imported but never exported* (sheets-catalog.js:219), so it resolves to `undefined` and bounds nothing today. Autobase is a linearized multi-writer op-log with no horizontal partitioning. It is the right primitive for **small, signed, slowly-changing descriptor sets** (catalog rows, shard manifests, trust edges — hundreds to low-thousands of rows) and the wrong primitive for millions of postings.
3. **`identity.signForApp` does NOT bind to drive ownership or to the user's root/social-graph identity.** Verified: the sub-key is `ed25519(SHA-256(rootSeed ‖ "pear-app-v1:" ‖ driveKeyHex))` (identity.js:239-247). Its pubkey has zero cryptographic relationship to (a) the Hyperdrive's signing key or (b) the root pubkey the Contacts graph is keyed on. Every trust/anti-spam claim that leans on "the posting's writerKey is the drive owner" or "the signer is a graph member" is **invalid until an explicit binding record is published**. This is the single most common factual error across proposals 1, 2, and 4.

**The synthesized design** (codename **Lighthouse**) is a hybrid that takes the best-scoring, best-grounded pieces of each:

- **Federation-of-signed-indexes** as the frame (Trustweave/HiveSearch/Constellation all converge here; it is the Almonit "client-side search over a curated signed set" pattern — the only model that historically worked).
- **Social/personal-first as the bootstrap and the structural Sybil gate** (Constellation, the highest-scored proposal): your own browsed/bookmarked content gives instant cold-start; a fake index is invisible until a real graph member endorses it.
- **The sharded-Hyperbee inverted index as the *full-text data structure*, but local-first and relay-pinned, NEVER as a global DHT ranking authority** (HiveSearch's genuinely-sound part: `t!<term>!<invScore>!<docId>` keys give top-k in one range scan).
- **A small signed descriptor room (schema-sheets/Autobee) for the federation directory and shard manifests** — reusing the just-shipped collaborative-catalog work verbatim.
- **HiveRelay as the durability floor, not the crawler and not the ranking authority** (all four converge).
- **One new, mandatory primitive that all of them needed and none correctly specified: a signed identity-binding record** linking root pubkey → per-app search sub-key → (optionally) drive control.

We ship **discovery first** (intent→key over signed descriptor rooms + personal index), prove it end-to-end on infrastructure already merged, and treat the sharded full-text layer and any relay query fast-path as later, independently-flagged increments.

## 2. Problem statement: why P2P search is hard, and the keyless-discovery problem

### 2.1 The keyless-naming problem (discovery)
Content on Pear is addressed by opaque 32-byte keys (`hyper://<hex|z32>`). There is no DNS, no central crawler, no naming authority. **Zooko's triangle** states a name cannot be simultaneously secure, decentralized, and human-meaningful. A Hyperdrive key is secure + decentralized but *not* human-meaningful. "Discovery" is the problem of supplying the missing human-meaningful corner: turning "the P2P blog about Hypercore" into the right 32-byte key.

The critical insight (shared by all four proposals' discoveryDesign sections, and correct): **the human-meaningful corner is supplied by a signed assertion from someone — a publisher self-listing, a curator listing, or a friend endorsing — that binds {human title/terms} → {32-byte key}.** Resolution is *relative to a trust context*, not globally unique. "find pearmail" resolves to whichever `pearmail` *your* trust graph vouches for. Naming is emergent, carried on signed rows, never a global registrar (which would re-create DNS/an authority).

### 2.2 The full-text problem (classic search)
Independent of naming, users want ranked retrieval over the *bodies* of P2P-published pages. This is where every hard P2P-search problem lives at once:

- **Multi-keyword AND scaling.** A k-word query must intersect k posting lists. In a term-sharded distributed index each term lives on a different host set; the scatter-gather + Zipf-hotspot cost is what killed YaCy. (TermShard's declared killer; review verdict: unproven, does not hold.)
- **Ranking without authority.** No central PageRank. Local page features (tf, title, meta) are publisher-controlled and trivially gamed (YaCy's downfall).
- **Sybil/spam.** Anyone can mint keys and publish content. Open writes invite flooding; curated writes cap coverage and re-create a soft authority.
- **Query privacy.** Sending a query to any non-local index leaks intent. A DHT fan-out leaks *which term-prefix shards* you join (8–12 bits/term) and the term-hash to the host.
- **Freshness vs durability.** "The index disappeared" (volunteer churn — YaCy; single operator — ipfs-search) vs "the index is a stale/poisoned snapshot."
- **Who stores the index, and why.** The incentive vacuum is empirically fatal; token economies reintroduce pay-to-rank corruption.

**These two engines are different and both needed.** Discovery is a directory problem solvable now; full-text is the research frontier. Conflating them — building one global inverted index and calling it "search" — is the trap.

## 4. Lessons from prior art — what to avoid

- **YaCy** (open, volunteer, global distributed inverted index): died on four things — (1) multi-keyword AND scatter-gather + Zipf hotspots; (2) trivially-gamed local-page-feature ranking; (3) plaintext queries shipped to a remote Solr (query-privacy leak); (4) "who keeps the index alive" under volunteer churn. **Avoid:** a global term-sharded ranking authority; ranking from publisher-controlled page features; plaintext query to any non-local index; durability that depends on volunteer goodwill.
- **ipfs-search** (central crawler over IPFS): worked until its single operator ran out of funding. **Avoid:** single-operator durability/crawl dependence. Make shards re-hostable by key and the directory multi-relay.
- **Almonit** (~100-site curated, client-side search over a signed set): the only model that *shipped usable, spam-free search* — but tiny coverage, soft central curation. **Adopt** the client-side-search-over-curated-signed-set pattern; **fix** its ceiling by making the federation itself P2P, signed, durable, and socially extensible.
- **Presearch** (token, keyword-staking): reintroduced pay-to-rank. **NFT-gated nodes:** recentralization. **Avoid:** any incentive that rewards rank, or any sellable/transferable asset.
- **Freenet** (social-graph routing): filter-bubble / popularity-cartel ossification. **Mitigate:** non-ossifying bootstrap trust set, exploration injection, easy un-following.
- **SybilGuard/SybilLimit/MeritRank/EigenTrust:** Sybils are poorly connected to the honest graph; bound influence by *attack edges*, not nodes. **Adopt** as the ranking/Sybil substrate — *conditional on* a working identity binding so "distinct endorser" is unfakeable.

## 5. The design space — four proposals compared

| | **TermShard** | **Trustweave** | **HiveSearch** | **Constellation** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Thesis** | True term-sharded distributed inverted index (YaCy done right) + relay-pinned durability | Federation of curated signed schema-sheets index rooms, client-side trust-ranked | Relay-hosted sharded Hyperbee inverted index + doc-manifest room, client-verified & re-ranked | Per-user signed personal index, fan-out over social graph |
| **Index unit** | One Hyperbee per term-prefix DHT shard | One schema-sheets room per curator | Sharded Hyperbee (index) + Autobase doc-manifest room | One Hyperbee per user |
| **Sharded by** | Term prefix | Curator | Term prefix (index) / doc (claimed) | Author |
| **Ranking** | Trust + BM25 + anchor proof, deterministic | Personalized PageRank over trust rooms | BM25 × personal MeritRank, deterministic | Social proximity × endorser breadth, deterministic |
| **Sybil gate** | Signed postings + anchor + trust distance | Signed rows + per-writer drop + coList | Per-app sig + trust gating + verification tier | Structural: invisible until a real contact endorses |
| **Query privacy** | Weak: prefix + termHash leak to host | Strong: local-first JMESPath | Local-first default / opt-in relay fast-path | Local-first; block-pattern leak to trusted peer |
| **Durability** | Relay-pin shards | Relay-pin rooms | Relay-pin shards (relay is also crawler) | Relay-pin personal indexes |
| **Cold-start** | Seed crawl frontier | Soft authority (default curators) | Soft authority (relay-listed) | **Your own data (trivial)** + starter room |
| **Killer risk** | Cross-shard AND at interactive latency | Curator coverage cold-start | Trust-graph→relay recentralization | Coverage starvation in sparse young graph |
| **Showstoppers (review)** | signForApp not drive-bound; AND unproven; "zero new code" overstated | Open room membership; identity binding absent; `list()` full-scan | **doc-manifest in Autobase can't scale**; `MAX_SHEETS_ROWS` unexported | **posting provenance unverifiable vs graph**; relay pins Hyperdrives not Hyperbees |
| **Score** | **4** | **4.5** | **5.5** | **6** |

### Key tradeoffs the table encodes
- **Decentralization ↔ AND-scaling tax.** The more the index is a global term-sharded structure, the harder interactive multi-term AND becomes. TermShard maximizes decentralization and inherits YaCy's scaling killer.
- **Openness ↔ Sybil-resistance.** Trustweave assumed curator-owned rooms but **schema-sheets membership is open** (`join()` optimistic-append auto-promotes any key-holder to writer — verified at sheets-bundle.cjs membership path); spam lands *inside* "trusted" rooms.
- **Cold-start ↔ centralization.** Every relevance prior collapses, for a new user, onto the bootstrap set. HiveSearch and Constellation both name this as their *own* killer risk: the relay/starter "default-trusted" set silently becomes the authority. Constellation alone has a real cold-start answer (your own data), which is why it scores highest.
- **Index placement ↔ privacy ↔ freshness/completeness.** Local-first leaks nothing but can't hold the long tail. Relay fast-path is fresh and fast but leaks the plaintext query. DHT is fresh-ish but a non-durable hint. No single placement is private, fresh, and complete at once.

**Why no proposal wins outright:** TermShard's AND fan-out is unproven and its provenance is forgeable; Trustweave and HiveSearch both stake their corpus on the one primitive (Autobase/schema-sheets) that cannot shard, and both rely on an identity binding that doesn't exist; Constellation has the best cold-start and the cleanest structural Sybil story but the same broken provenance binding and a coverage ceiling. **The win is a hybrid that takes Constellation's bootstrap + Sybil frame, HiveSearch's sharded-Hyperbee full-text data structure, Trustweave/HiveSearch's signed-descriptor-room federation, and fixes the shared showstoppers (identity binding, corpus-not-in-Autobase, local-first-by-default).**

## 6. Recommended architecture — "Lighthouse" (synthesized hybrid), end to end

### 6.1 The frame
Search is a **federation of small, owner-published, signed, queryable indexes** that the browser **replicates and ranks client-side over a personal trust graph**, with HiveRelay as the durability floor. There is **no global term-sharded DHT ranking authority** and **no per-document corpus in Autobase**. Three index *tiers*, each a distinct primitive used for what it is good at:

1. **Personal index (per user)** — the bootstrap, the cold-start answer, and the structural Sybil gate. A Hyperbee of what you browse/bookmark/endorse. (Constellation.)
2. **Descriptor rooms (federation directory + shard manifests + trust edges)** — small signed schema-sheets/Autobee rooms, ≤ low-thousands of rows, the just-shipped collaborative-catalog substrate. The discovery/naming layer. (Trustweave/HiveSearch, bounded to where Autobase is sound.)
3. **Full-text shards (per publisher/crawler)** — sharded Hyperbees `t!<term>!<invScore>!<docId>`, replicated locally for the query terms, relay-pinned for durability. The deferred, flag-gated, research-grade layer. (HiveSearch's sound piece, used local-first, never as a global DHT authority.)

### 6.3 Exact key schemas

**(A) Personal index — one Hyperbee per user, two co-located sub-spaces in ONE core (never one core per term — fatal for Corestore):**
```
t!<term>!<invScore>!<docId>   -> { docId, driveKey, path, field:'title'|'body',
                                    tf, titleHit:bool, signerPubkey, sig, publishedAt }
        invScore = String(Number.MAX_SAFE_INTEGER - localScore).padStart(18,'0')   // top-k via forward scan
d!<docId>                     -> { docId, driveKey, path, title, terms:[...],
                                    visibility:'shared'|'endorsed'|'private',
                                    endorseSig, contentHash, publishedAt }
        docId = sha256(driveKey ‖ '\0' ‖ path).slice(0,16) hex          // stable, dedup-friendly
meta!owner   -> <rootPubkey>
meta!binding -> { rootPubkey, searchSubPubkey, sig_by_root }            // THE binding (§6.4)
meta!version -> 2
```
Multi-term AND = bounded client-side intersection of *small pre-filtered per-author candidate lists* (top-N per term per author), **never a federation-wide intersection**. This is the deliberate answer to the AND-scaling tax: intersect only small, already-trust-bounded sets.

**(B) Descriptor rooms — schema-sheets/Autobee, bounded row counts. Three new schemas alongside the shipped `apps`:**
```
resource  : { title<=200, hyperUrl<=300 | driveKey ^[0-9a-f]{64}$,
              kind enum[site,drive,app,dataset,feed,room,agent],
              terms array<string<=40> maxItems 64, description<=1000, lang<=8,
              contentHash ^[0-9a-f]{64}$, crawledAt int,
              verification enum[unverified,relay-listed,author-signed,multi-curator] }
              required:[title]; anyOf:[driveKey|hyperUrl]
trust     : { subject ^[0-9a-f]{64}$ (curator/contact root pubkey),
              weight int -100..100, tags array<string<=40>, revoked bool }
index-shard (manifest): { shardId 2-4hex, shardKey ^[0-9a-f]{64}$ (Hyperbee key),
              termRangeLo, termRangeHi, docCount int, treeHash ^[0-9a-f]{64}$,
              treeLength int, epoch int, crawlerPubkey, sig }   required:[shardId,shardKey,epoch]
```
Every row carries `memberkey` (built-in) **and** a detached `signForApp` attestation binding `{signerPubkey, row, roomKey, schemaVersion}` — verified against the IdentityBinding at rank time, **not** trusted via `memberkey` (because membership is open).

**(C) Full-text shards (deferred tier) — one physical Hyperbee per term-prefix, fixed fan-out (256 → 4096):**
```
shardId = sha256(term)[0:1]               // 2 hex = 256 shards; bump to 3-hex past ~10M docs
t!<termHash16>!<scoreInv32be>!<docId16>  -> { driveKey, path, tf, positions? }
df!<termHash16>                          -> document-frequency counter (IDF)
meta!docCount / meta!epoch
```
A shard is ONE Hyperbee. The **doc-manifest is a docId-sharded Hyperbee** keyed by `docId` for point-gets — **NOT** an Autobase room (the HiveSearch showstopper fix). Autobase descriptor rooms hold only the *shard manifests* (hundreds of rows), never per-document rows.

**(D) HyperDHT pointers — tiny signed hints, 48h TTL, never storage:**
```
mutablePut(rootKeypair, { indexKey:32B, seq, sig })          // contact → personal-index key
mutablePut(roomKeypair, { directoryRoomKey:32B, epoch })     // bootstrap directory hint
immutablePut(canonical IdentityBinding)                       // content-addressed binding pin
```

### 6.4 The new mandatory primitive: IdentityBinding
The single fix that unblocks proposals 1, 2, and 4. Publish once, into `meta!binding` of the personal index *and* the DHT:
```
IdentityBinding = { rootPubkey, searchSubPubkey, sig_by_root }
  where searchSubPubkey = identity.getAppKeypair('search').publicKey
        sig_by_root      = identity.sign(canonical({rootPubkey, searchSubPubkey, 'lighthouse-binding-v1'}))
```
Add `identity.verify(payload, sig, pubkey)` (absent today — `sodium.crypto_sign_verify_detached`). At rank time the RowVerifier:
1. resolves the signer's `searchSubPubkey` from a posting,
2. checks the IdentityBinding signed by a `rootPubkey` **that is in the trust frontier**,
3. requires the posting's `signForApp('search', …)` signature to verify against that bound subkey.

Only then does "distinct endorser," "frontier membership," and "drop-a-writer-wholesale" become enforceable. For **publisher self-listing** (proving drive control, not just identity), require an in-drive attestation: the publisher writes `drive.put('/.well-known/lighthouse-attest', sig)` and the verifier fetches it — only such drive-anchored rows earn `author-signed` tier. Third-party (proxy-tap / crawler) postings are `unverified`/`relay-listed` hints, rank-capped by default.

### 6.5 The query path (step by step)
1. User types intent. QueryPlanner tokenizes into whitelisted terms through the `safeJmesPath` chokepoint (never raw-text-concat).
2. TrustFrontier built from Contacts (root-pubkey) + swarm-grant tiers: hop-0 = {me}, hop-1 = contacts, hop-2 = contacts-of-contacts (lazy, capped). **Fan-out is bounded to a trust-ranked subset** — this is the deliberate guard against YaCy's scatter-gather.
3. **hop-0 always answers locally** from the personal index (zero network, zero leak). This guarantees a non-empty result on day one — the cold-start win.
4. For each frontier peer: resolve their personal-index key (DHT pointer / contact-record fallback, batch-resolved at app start not per query), replicate read-only, run `t!<term>!` range scans + small intersection **on device**.
5. For descriptor rooms: on-device JMESPath over `resource` rows (intent→key directory). For the full-text tier (when enabled): replicate only the query-term shards locally and scan.
6. **RowVerifier** drops everything that fails IdentityBinding + signForApp + frontier-membership + (for shards) shard-membership proof, *before* ranking.
7. After a **bounded collection window** (e.g. 600ms or N peers), the **pure deterministic Ranker** scores the assembled candidate set. No wall-clock in the fold (autobee-catalog-apply.cjs discipline); freshness uses a query-time constant passed in, kept out of the per-doc fold.
8. Dedup by `driveKey+path` (getAggregatedApps discipline). Render ranked `hyper://` keys with **provenance chips** ("endorsed by 3 friends incl. Maya", verification tier, "ranked by your trust graph vs default relay").
9. Click resolves through the **P2P-first** proxy (the relay race flipped — see §9).

## 7. Indexing & freshness

**Crawl is a byproduct of browsing.** The PersonalIndexer subscribes to the existing `addBookmark`/`addEndorsement` hooks; for opt-in/endorsed pages it tokenizes the HTML body **hyper-proxy already fetched** (`_doHybridFetch` has the bytes in memory) — lowercase, NFKC, stopword-strip, cap ~64 terms/doc, top-N postings/term. Raw private history is **never** published by default. This structurally fixes YaCy's volunteer-crawl shortfall: every browse contributes, marginal cost ≈ tokenizing bytes already in memory.

**Three on-ramps for keyless content** (so a fresh key becomes findable without DNS/registry): (1) **passive** — anyone who browses a drive indexes it; (2) **active self-list** — the publisher writes a `resource` row + the in-drive `/.well-known/lighthouse-attest` (earns `author-signed`); (3) **link-graph expansion** — postings record outbound `hyper://` anchors, walked transitively from any seed.

**Incremental freshness is a diff, not a re-scan.** Each Hyperdrive/Hyperbee exposes a version; the crawler stores last-seen version and uses `createDiffStream` (confirmed present in the bundle) to pull only deltas. Re-query across sessions pulls only the personal-index delta. For the shard tier, postings carry `driveVersion`; supersession is resolved at read time (newest version wins, mirroring getAggregatedApps version-wins dedup); shards are append-only, so **epoch compaction** periodically rebuilds into fresh cores dropping superseded postings and advertises a bumped `index-shard` row.

**Stale-snapshot detection.** A relay serving epoch N when the capability doc says N+2, or a `treeLength` regression vs the `index-shard` row, is a stale signal that docks the relay's freshness reputation. Clients compare served `treeLength` against the signed manifest every query.

**Storage discipline.** The existing `STORAGE_LIMIT`/eviction must be taught to (a) pin shard cores with in-flight queries (never evict mid-query), and (b) LRU-trim the personal index with hard total-doc/byte caps mirroring `MAX_HISTORY`. Recall loss is acceptable for a rediscovery engine.

## 8. Ranking & sybil/spam resistance

**Ranking is a PURE function** computed after a bounded window over the **verified** candidate set + a **static** trust-graph snapshot — so a relay returning junk cannot move ordering, and orderings never flicker run-to-run (the autobee no-wall-clock discipline). Determinism is per-fixed-input; note honestly it does *not* imply global agreement (two users score over different replicated graphs — that is correct and intended for a *personalized* engine).

```
score(doc) = w1·socialProximity(1/(hop+1))          // hop-1 friend beats hop-2
           × w2·endorserBreadth(log(1+distinct frontier endorsers))   // near-unfakeable IF binding holds
           × w3·tierWeight(swarm-grant A/B/C)
           × w4·termMatch(BM25 from posting tf + df/docCount + length)  // none publisher-asserted in a gameable field
           × w5·freshness(exp(-age/halflife))
```

**Sybil/spam resistance — layered, structural, all conditional on IdentityBinding (§6.4):**
1. **Structural invisibility (Constellation, the strongest idea).** A Sybil's index is invisible until a real graph member endorses it — and endorsing is a signed QR/invite ceremony, not a free action. `endorserBreadth` counts *distinct frontier root-pubkeys*; an attacker behind one honest edge cannot manufacture breadth (SybilLimit: bound attack edges, not nodes).
2. **Every posting/row signed**, verified against the bound subkey of a frontier member. Anonymous spam is impossible.
3. **Drop-a-writer-wholesale.** Remove a contact / curator edge → their entire index leaves your frontier at read-time set-union, no rewrite (real Corestore per-writer-core capability).
4. **Verification enum gate** (`unverified` < `relay-listed` < `author-signed` < `multi-curator`); users can hard-filter to ≥ a tier.
5. **coList bonus** — a key independently listed by mutually-trusted curators is the strongest honest signal — *but* only as strong as the trust graph is Sybil-resistant; on a fresh user it amplifies the bootstrap set, so it is rank-capped until the graph grows.
6. **Page-controlled features carry ZERO weight.** Only signed social/term-presence facts rank — exactly what YaCy lacked.

**Honest residual weaknesses** (per reviews): trust-graph **cold-start** (a new user's seed is the baked-in set → soft authority); **in-graph spam** from a compromised/careless contact (reactive removal only — mitigate with per-contact rate caps + tier weighting + "mute this endorser" UI + require k≥2 distinct endorsers for hop≥2 results); **anchor proofs** prove term-occurrence not relevance (a spammer who really publishes a keyword-stuffed drive passes) — so anchor raises cost to "one published drive per spam cluster," and trust-rank caps the unknown publisher.

## 9. Query privacy

**Local-first by construction is the default and only v1 posture.** To search a peer's index you *replicate* their (small) index and run the scan **on device**; the query string never goes on the wire. This is the property YaCy lost by shipping plaintext to Solr — we never make that call. hop-0 (own index) leaks nothing, ever.

**Mandatory prerequisite — flip the proxy P2P-first.** Verified: `_doHybridFetch` races `Promise.any([relay, p2p])` (hyper-proxy.js:686), leaking nearly every fetch — including post-query click-through — to the default-on relay. Make the search/verify/click path **P2P-first**, relay only after a P2P timeout, never for keys flagged private. Without this, a private query's *destination* still leaks. (All four proposals correctly diagnose this; it is unbuilt and is a hard prerequisite, not infrastructure-on-hand.)

**Residual metadata leaks (honest, bounded):**
- **Block-request pattern.** A sparse Hyperbee range scan fetches the B-tree path blocks for your term, so the *serving* peer can correlate offsets to a scanned term-prefix. Mitigation: default to **batch-replicating a peer's whole (small) index** and scanning fully locally (tier-2), keeping per-query sparse scans the exception; the served party is by definition a trusted contact, bounding the adversary. Index-size caps keep full replication cheap. Do **not** advertise this as "perfect" privacy — it is metadata-leaky to a trusted party, better than YaCy, not a guarantee.
- **DHT pointer lookups** reveal which contacts you resolve. Mitigation: resolve the whole frontier's pointers once at app start, prefer the contact-record fallback (no DHT hit for established contacts).
- **Shard-set fingerprinting** (deferred full-text tier): joining the discoveryKeys of exactly your query-term shards reveals the term set. Mitigation: warm hot-cache of common shards always joined; rotating cover-set; document that local-first hides query *text* but not a coarse query-*topic* signal.

**Opt-in relay fast-path is OFF by default, clearly labeled "shares your query with <relay>," rate-limited.** Hashed terms (salted-per-epoch sha256) resist casual logging but are **dictionary-attackable** for common terms — stated plainly; decoy padding only raises guessing cost linearly. **The UI must never let a user believe the fast path is private.** PIR / oblivious-shard-fetch is a v3 research hook: design the shard RPC as fetch-by-key so it can slot in later; do not block v1 on it.

## 10. Incentives, durability & HiveRelay's role

**Durability does NOT depend on volunteer goodwill** — the lesson from YaCy (churn) and ipfs-search (single operator). The **durability floor lives on HiveRelay**: every personal index, descriptor room, and full-text shard is pinned via `hiveRelay.seed(key,{replicas:3})` the moment it is published (the exact path at index.js:1592; the dev catalogue already does this for an Autobase room at index.js:1566). Relays are the floor, **not** the crawler and **not** the ranking authority.

**Verified durability caveat (Constellation/Trustweave showstopper).** The relay's *active offline-serving* path is Hyperdrive-shaped (`new Hyperdrive(store, key)`, `getDriveSize` reads metaBytes+blobBytes, AutoHeal recruits replicas). A bare Hyperbee/Autobase core has no blobs core and a different manifest, so it gets **best-effort raw-core seeding via `store.replicate`, not AutoHealed size-capped pinning**. **Resolution (pick per tier):** (a) add a first-class "seed raw core" mode to HiveRelay (no Hyperdrive wrapper, no blob size-check) — the clean fix; or (b) publish each index's materialized snapshot as a plain Hyperdrive the relay pins natively, treating the live log as the authoring layer; or (c) lower the claim to "querier-as-transient-seeder + incidental corestore replication" and design around offline gaps. The roadmap routes through (a) for shards and (c)+querier-seeding for personal indexes initially.

**Why peers participate (incentives aligned by design, not bolted on with a token):**
- **You build your own index because it is your offline-first search** — the cold-start-via-own-data property *is* the incentive (works with zero peers).
- **You serve friends because reciprocity is social and visible** — "endorsed by Maya" makes contributing a low-cost observable social good among people who know each other.
- **Relays pin because it is the same `seed()` path they already run** for content availability; a broadly-pinning relay becomes the cold-start directory source (reputational pull).
- **Every querier who replicated an index becomes a transient seeder** (BitTorrent-style: popularity = redundancy).

**Non-negotiable guardrails (Presearch/NFT lessons):** any reward attaches **only to durability/seeding/uptime**, measured by **PoR-style freshness challenges** ("return `t!` at key K from shard P at length ≥ L, crawled within T" → Hyperbee proof; failed audit docks reputation, demotes in bootstrap), **never to rank**; any credit is **non-transferable, expiring**, redeemed only as elevated swarm-grant standing / rate-limit headroom — **never a sellable asset, never pay-to-rank, never NFT-gated nodes**. Clients re-verify every relay claim, so a captured relay degrades availability but **cannot forge or censor undetectably** (omission still needs a completeness anchor — see §13).

**Honest residual:** the **cold long-tail** (prefixes/indexes nobody queries) has weak incentive and falls to relays, re-concentrating power (the ipfs-search risk). Mitigations (re-hostable-by-key, multi-relay directory) bound but do not resolve it.

## 13. Open research problems

1. **Cross-shard multi-keyword AND at interactive latency over Zipf-distributed posting lists on independent volunteer hosts** — the determinative, unproven question (TermShard's and the brief's #4). Needs measurement on real corpora; bigram/term-pair co-located shards may convert head-of-distribution cross-host AND into single-host AND.
2. **Completeness / anti-omission under a captured relay or host** — re-verification proves authenticity, not completeness. Per-epoch signed Bloom/MPHF commitments make omission sample-detectable, but a robust, cheap completeness proof is open.
3. **Trust-graph cold-start without a soft authority** — personalized rank collapses to the bootstrap set for a new user. Auto-edge-from-normal-use + multi-relay swappable bootstrap soften it; a principled non-ossifying bootstrap is unsolved.
4. **Query privacy beyond local-first** — hashed terms are dictionary-reversible; the relay fast-path is honestly-not-private. Single-server PIR / oblivious shard fetch is the research lane; cost is currently prohibitive.
5. **Long-tail durability without re-concentration** — absent a token, prefixes nobody queries fall to relays. Incentives that reward *seeding only* (non-transferable, expiring) need validation that they actually keep the cold tail alive.
6. **Filter-bubble vs coverage** in social-graph rank — exploration injection vs cartel ossification (the Freenet tension).
7. **The canonical bootstrap relay z32 / directory room** — the still-blocking shared operator question, identical to the catalog's open TBD.
8. **Autobase writer/op-log scaling ceilings** — Holepunch publishes no hard limits; the descriptor-room row-count budget where `list()` materialization becomes painful needs measurement (informs where the Autobase/Hyperbee boundary sits).

### TermShard: a HyperDHT-native, signed, relay-durable distributed inverted index

**Thesis.** Build a true term-sharded distributed inverted index — YaCy's architecture, but with the four things YaCy lacked: signed postings (per-app derived keys we already ship), anchor-proof anti-spam (re-verify the term against the actual drive@version), trust-weighted deterministic ranking over our existing identity/grant graph, and — the decisive fix — RELAY-PINNED DURABILITY so a shard never dies with its volunteer host. The term space is partitioned into a few thousand prefix shards; each shard is a single Hyperbee hosted by volunteer peers and discovered via a deterministic Hyperswarm topic, with HyperDHT mutable records as tiny freshness HINTS, never storage. Crawling is a free byproduct of browsing (the proxy already fetches the bytes), so the keyless web indexes itself and grows along the published link graph. We embrace maximum decentralization and confront its two hard costs honestly: multi-keyword AND fan-out (YaCy's scaling killer, bounded here with Bloom co-term prefilters and trusted-host fan-out) and query privacy (hash-only-on-wire + cover terms + local-first, materially better than YaCy but explicitly not solved — PIR is left as a v3 hook). This is the high-decentralization counter-proposal to the brief's curated-federation prior; its viability rests entirely on making AND queries feel instant.

**Index.** SHARD = one Hyperbee per 12-bit term-prefix (4096 shards; start 8-bit/256). Inside a shard's Hyperbee, ONE physical core (never one core per term — that is fatal for Corestore), with these key spaces (all keys are utf8-sortable so range scans work):

POSTINGS: `post!<termHash_hex>!<score_desc_u32_hex>!<driveKey_hex>` → value `{driveKey, driveVersion, tf, fieldFlags(bit0=title,bit1=h1,bit2=h2,bit3=body,bit4=link-anchor), title:<=200, snippet:<=300, lang, crawledAt, writerKey, sig, sigVersion}`. The `<score_desc>` segment is a precomputed host-local score inverted (0xFFFFFFFF - score) so a forward range scan yields highest-score-first WITHOUT a reverse cursor; this is the AND-prefilter feed. A SECOND index `byd!<termHash_hex>!<driveKey_hex>` → seq-pointer enforces one-posting-per-(term,drive) so re-crawls UPSERT (delete old score-key, put new) instead of accumulating.

SHARD META: `meta!schema` → schema/version; `meta!prefix` → the 12-bit prefix it serves; `meta!crawledThrough` → highest driveVersion folded; `meta!writers!<writerKey_hex>` → `{rep, droppedAt|null, postingCount}` (per-writer accounting so a bad writer is dropped wholesale by a single range-delete of `byd!*` filtered on writerKey — the read-time set-union/exclusion the brief calls for). `bloom!<termHash_hex>` → a serialized Bloom filter of the driveKeys that have this term, so a client can ship the co-term Bloom and the host intersects cheaply.

WHO STORES IT: any opted-in peer hosts one or more prefixes (capacity-bounded). Multiple hosts per prefix = a replica set; each rebuilds independently from (a) signed postings pushed by crawlers on the shard topic and (b) `createDiffStream` backfill from a peer host. DURABILITY: the shard core key is pinned on HiveRelay (`hiveRelay.seed`), so the prefix survives total volunteer churn — the answer YaCy and ipfs-search never had.

POINTER LAYER (DHT): `dht.mutablePut(hostKeypair, value=canonical({shardCoreKey, prefix, length, crawledThrough, capacityFree}))` — value <1KB, signed by HyperDHT's mutable-record signature, 48h TTL, refreshed every 20min. Clients `mutableGet` by host announce key discovered via the swarm topic; the DHT is ONLY a freshness/bootstrap hint. `immutablePut(canonicalShardManifest)` gives a content-addressed pin a relay can re-serve.

UPDATE/FRESHNESS: event-driven re-crawl on version bump → upsert supersedes stale posting; `meta!crawledThrough` advances; `createDiffStream(prevLength)` propagates only deltas to replicas/verifiers. No global rebuild ever runs.

**Ranking / anti-sybil.** Ranking is a PURE, DETERMINISTIC function f(candidateSet, staticTrustGraph) computed AFTER a bounded collection window — never incrementally as host responses stream in (kills YaCy's arrival-time flicker). Score per (driveKey, query) = w1·trust(publisherKey) + w2·Σ_term termScore(tf, fieldFlags, idf-from-shard-bloom-cardinality) + w3·anchorConfidence + w4·link-in-degree(driveKey) − w5·spamPenalty, with fixed weights baked into the client (not publisher-controlled).

TRUST (the Sybil answer): trust(publisherKey) is a web-of-trust rank over the LOCAL signed graph we already have — the user's identity, their per-app trust tiers, their swarm-grant standing, and edges harvested from drives they themselves visited/trusted. We run a personalized EigenTrust/MeritRank-style propagation seeded from {self, default-trusted publishers, relay-listed publishers}: Sybils are structurally far from the honest seed set and get near-zero weight. CRITICALLY, ranking inputs are SIGNED facts (publisherKey from signForApp, attestations, relay-listing), never publisher-declared page features — so a spammer cannot stuff tf, fake a title, or self-assign rank.

ANCHOR PROOF (anti-spam at the data layer): every posting names `driveVersion`. A verifier (or the host, lazily, or the client on the top-K only) re-fetches `hyper://driveKey@driveVersion/index.html` and confirms the term actually occurs at the claimed field. A posting that fails its anchor is dropped and its writerKey's `rep` is docked; repeated failures → the writer's whole contribution core is excluded by the host (`meta!writers` droppedAt set, range-delete). This makes term-stuffing/keyword-spam non-free: you must actually publish a drive containing the term, and even then trust-rank caps an unknown publisher.

RATE/COST: per-writer posting quotas enforced by the host (signed postings make the writer attributable); pushing N postings costs N signatures and consumes the writer's swarm-grant rate budget (reusing the existing per-app rate limits). SYBIL RESISTANCE SUMMARY: identity-cost (deriving distinct trusted sub-keys is cheap but UNtrusted sub-keys earn ~0 rank) + anchor-proof (must really host the content) + trust-graph distance (poorly-connected Sybils sink) + per-writer-core isolation (drop the whole set). Open weakness: the trust seed set is a soft authority and has cold-start (a brand-new user trusts only the default seed); honest-but-obscure content under-ranks until it accrues edges.

**Discovery.** Two senses of discovery, both addressed. (A) DISCOVERY-AS-SEARCH (intent→key): a keyword query returns ranked hyper:// keys with human titles/snippets pulled from postings — this IS the naming/directory layer (Zooko's triangle resolved at query time: the key stays secure+decentralized, the human-meaningful title rides along in the signed posting). The first time ANY user browses a drive, the proxy-tap crawls it and pushes postings, so a freshly-published drive becomes findable as soon as one peer visits it — no central crawler, no DNS.

(B) BOOTSTRAPPING KEYLESS CONTENT INTO THE INDEX: a publisher does not need to register anywhere. Three on-ramps: (1) PASSIVE — anyone who opens the drive crawls it (the common case). (2) ACTIVE SELF-SUBMIT — the publisher's own client, knowing its driveKey, crawls its own drive and pushes signed postings (so a publisher seeds their own discoverability, and the signature proves they are the drive's per-app key). (3) LINK-GRAPH EXPANSION — postings record outbound hyper:// links (fieldFlags link-anchor); a crawler that indexes drive A enqueues A's outbound drive keys for crawl, so the index grows along the published web graph from any seed — this is how the keyless web is walked without DNS.

SEED SET: ship a baked-in bootstrap of {the canonical schema-sheets catalog room's app driveKeys, a small default-trusted publisher list, the relay-listed set} as the initial crawl frontier AND the initial trust seed, so a cold install has both something to search and a trust anchor. NAMING: titles are advisory and signed-by-crawler-context, not authoritative; collisions are fine because the canonical identity is always the 32-byte key — the title just makes it human-findable. The STILL-OPEN question (shared with the catalog) is which relay z32 / bootstrap room is the canonical baked-in seed.

**Privacy.** QUERY PRIVACY is the sharpest weakness of a DHT-native fan-out index and we confront it head-on rather than pretending it is solved. Threat: to query term T a client must (a) join `shardTopic(prefix(T))` — revealing INTEREST IN A PREFIX (256–4096 buckets, so ~8–12 bits of a term per query) to the shard hosts and to DHT-path observers, and (b) send `termHash` to the host, revealing the exact term to that host.

MITIGATIONS, layered:
1) HASH-ONLY ON THE WIRE: hosts only ever see `termHash = blake2b(term)`, never plaintext (unlike YaCy's plaintext Solr). A host learns which term-hashes are popular but cannot trivially read the query unless it dictionary-reverses common terms (which it can — so this is necessary-not-sufficient).
2) PREFIX COARSENING + COVER TERMS: the client joins a prefix that aggregates many terms, and pads each real query with K decoy term-hashes drawn to share the same prefixes, so a host sees a set it cannot distinguish real-from-decoy within. Default K=2–4 (tunable; costs bandwidth).
3) BOUNDED FAN-OUT TO TRUSTED HOSTS FIRST: prefer hosts in the user's trust set / relay-listed hosts, minimizing exposure to arbitrary peers.
4) LOCAL-FIRST DEFAULT: a client that already hosts (or has cached) the relevant prefix shard answers from its OWN Hyperbee with ZERO network query — the same local-first posture the catalog already has (JMESPath on-device). Frequent users converge toward hosting their hot prefixes, leaking nothing.
5) RELAY FAST-PATH IS OPT-IN: the HTTP /search route leaks the full query to a semi-trusted relay; it is OFF by default and rate-limited, exactly because hyper-proxy.js:686 today over-leaks to the default-on relay — we make the search path P2P-first.
6) PIR IS A v3 RESEARCH HOOK, NOT v1: single-server PIR or oblivious shard fetch could remove the host-sees-termHash leak but is too costly now; we leave the shard interface PIR-shaped (fetch-by-key) so it can slot in later.

METADATA LEAKAGE that remains (honest): join-topic timing correlates a peer to a prefix; result clicks resolve a drive (visible to that drive's swarm); the decoy scheme costs bandwidth and a determined host doing traffic analysis across many queries can still profile a heavy user. We do NOT claim query privacy is solved — we claim it is materially better than YaCy and degrades gracefully toward local-first.

**Incentives.** WHY PEERS CRAWL: crawling is a BYPRODUCT, not a chore — the proxy-tap indexes content the user already fetched to browse, so the marginal cost is tokenization of bytes already in memory. Every user who browses contributes postings for free; this is the structural fix for YaCy's volunteer-crawl shortfall.

WHY PEERS HOST SHARDS: (a) self-interest — hosting your own hot prefixes makes YOUR queries local-first and private (mitigation #4 above is also the incentive); (b) reciprocity — the swarm-grant/trust system gives shard-hosting peers higher standing (more inbound replication, priority in others' fan-out); (c) it is cheap — a single Hyperbee, capacity-bounded, that you can drop anytime.

WHY RELAYS STORE/SERVE: the DURABILITY FLOOR lives on HiveRelay, not volunteers — `hiveRelay.seed(shardCoreKey)` pins shards so a prefix never disappears when its volunteer host churns. This deliberately concentrates durability on relays (the ipfs-search single-operator risk), MITIGATED by: shard cores are relay-agnostic (any relay or peer can re-host by key), clients re-verify every relay-served posting's signature/anchor (a captured relay can degrade availability but cannot forge), and the relay-directory lists multiple swappable relays.

DURABILITY MECHANICS: replica set ≥3 (volunteer hosts) + relay pin; `createDiffStream` keeps replicas convergent; a Proof-of-Retrievability challenge ("return post! at key K from shard P at length ≥ L crawled within T") lets the reputation system audit hosts/relays and dock failures.

INCENTIVE GUARDRAILS (non-negotiable): any reward is for DURABILITY/SEEDING, never for RANK (no pay-to-rank like Presearch; no NFT-gated nodes). If a credit exists it is a NON-TRANSFERABLE, EXPIRING swarm-grant standing redeemed as replication priority — never a sellable asset. The honest weakness: absent any token, hosting the cold long-tail (prefixes nobody queries) has weak incentive and may fall entirely to relays, re-concentrating power — this is the durability/centralization tension the brief flags, and it is unresolved.

**First slice.** SMALLEST SHIPPABLE SLICE — 'single-prefix local index + self-host query', 256-shard (8-bit) space, NO trust-rank, NO anchor-verify, NO relay fast-path yet:

1) CRAWLER TAP: in hyper-proxy.js, after a successful `_fetchP2P` of an index.html, fire a debounced background job (mirroring ensureDevCatalogue's best-effort off-critical-path style) that tokenizes the page and writes signed postings into a single local Hyperbee `store.namespace('search-contrib').get('postings-v1')`, key `post!<termHash>!<scoreDesc>!<driveKey>`, signed with `identity.signForApp(driveKey, canonical, 'search-posting-v1')`. This alone makes everything a user has browsed locally searchable, end-to-end, with real signatures.

2) LOCAL QUERY: a new CMD_SEARCH that tokenizes a structured query (through the existing safeJmesPath/whitelist chokepoint), runs per-term `bee.createReadStream({gte:'post!'+termHash+'!', lt:...'~', limit})` (the catalog-manager.js:165 idiom), intersects by driveKey for AND, and returns ranked {driveKey, title, snippet} using a simple tf+fieldFlags score (deterministic, pure). Wire it to the search box.

3) ONE SHARED SHARD OVER THE SWARM: pick ONE well-known prefix-0 shard topic; the dev/relay instance hosts it (`swarm.join(shardTopic(0), {server:true})`, Hyperbee, pinned via the existing pinDriveBestEffort/hiveRelay.seed). Clients `swarm.join(shardTopic(0), {client:true})`, push their prefix-0 postings to the host, and query it over a Protomux `search/query-v1` channel. This proves the full P2P loop (publish posting → host stores → remote client finds key) on infrastructure already in the tree, with exactly one shard.

That slice ships discovery (intent→key with human titles) over the real P2P stack, reusing the crawler tap, signForApp, Hyperbee range scans, swarm.join, and hiveRelay.seed — no new crypto, no new discovery layer, no DHT pointer layer yet. Trust-rank, anchor-proof, full 256/4096 sharding, the DHT pointer hints, cover-term privacy, and the opt-in relay /search route are the next increments.

**Killer risk.** The multi-keyword AND fan-out is the single thing most likely to kill this. A DHT-native term-sharded index means every keyword in a query lands on a DIFFERENT shard hosted by DIFFERENT peers, so an AND query is a scatter-gather that must intersect posting lists pulled from several independent hosts — the EXACT cost model that killed YaCy (slow multi-term joins + Zipf hotspots on popular terms + per-host latency variance). Bloom co-term prefilters, bounded fan-out, and inverted-score range keys reduce constant factors but do not change the fundamental shape: a 3-word query over a popular-term shard set can require fetching and intersecting large lists across the network within an interactive latency budget, and the hottest-prefix hosts become contention/centralization points. If cross-shard AND cannot be made to feel instant on common queries, users fall back to the relay (re-leaking privacy and re-centralizing), and the decentralized index becomes vestigial — precisely the failure mode the brief warns is the most determinative single decision (#4). This is why the brief's own strong prior is the curated-federation model; the DHT-native design must prove AND-at-interactive-latency or it dies here.

**Review — feasibility `partial`, score 4/10, confidence high — every load-bearing code citation was checked against the actual repo (identity.js:262 derivation, swarm-bridge.js limits, relay-client.js scope, catalog-manager.js range scans) and against the installed primitive versions (hyperdht 6.30.0, hyperswarm 4.17.0, hyperbee 2.27.3, autobase 7.27.3). The two showstoppers (forgeable publisher binding; unproven AND fan-out) are grounded, not speculative. Remaining uncertainty is only about achievable AND latency, which is unmeasured by definition..**

Showstoppers:
- signForApp does NOT bind to drive ownership — the posting 'publisherKey' is derived from the CRAWLER's seed, so self-submit cannot authenticate a publisher and any peer can forge postings for any driveKey. Every trust/anti-spam claim that leans on writerKey==drive-owner is invalid until a real drive-attestation mechanism (signed manifest inside the drive) is added.
- Cross-shard multi-keyword AND at interactive latency is unproven and is the design's own declared killer; on the evidence (Zipf posting lists, independent-host tail latency, multi-hundred-KB co-term Blooms) it does not hold, and failure routes users to the query-leaking, recentralizing relay fallback — collapsing the value proposition.
- The DHT pointer layer and the relay /search route are presented as 'zero new code' reusing existing infra, but neither exists in the repo (no mutablePut/Get anywhere; relay is Hyperdrive-file-serving only). The proposal materially overstates how much is already built — the new surface area is large, not incremental.

Top threats:
- **critical** — Posting forgery / impersonation: because signForApp(driveKey) is derived from the SIGNER's seed, not the drive's key, any peer can sign postings claiming any driveKey (good or malicious title/snippet/tf). A spammer can attach attractive titles to a victim's driveKey or flood postings for keys they do not own. → *Do NOT treat writerKey as drive-bound. Require the publisher self-submit on-ramp to sign with a key provably tied to drive control — e.g. publish a signed manifest INSIDE the drive (drive.put('/.well-known/search-attest', sig)) and have the host/verifier fetch it; only such drive-anchored postings get publisher trust. Treat all proxy-tap (third-party) postings as untrusted hints subject to anchor-check + trust-rank caps. Rank caps for unknown writers must be the default, not the exception.*
- **critical** — AND fan-out collapse → relay fallback → recentralization: if cross-shard AND is not interactive, users fall to the opt-in relay /search, which leaks the full plaintext query and concentrates both index and query-visibility on relay operators — the exact failure the brief warns is most determinative. → *Prove AND-at-interactive-latency on real Zipf-distributed corpora BEFORE building the DHT layer. Consider co-locating common term-pairs on the same shard (bigram shards) to convert cross-host AND into single-host AND for the head of the distribution. If it cannot be shown, the brief's curated-federation prior wins and this design should not ship.*
- **high** — Hot-prefix hotspots & host censorship: Zipf concentrates load and power on the few hosts of popular prefixes; a hostile host can silently return truncated/biased posting lists (it cannot forge signatures, but it can withhold). Detection needs the Proof-of-Retrievability audit, which is unbuilt. → *Mandate replica-set >=3 with cross-checking (client queries 2-3 hosts and diffs result sets; large divergence flags a withholder). Build the PoR challenge before relying on volunteer hosts. Accept that popular-prefix hosting is a soft-centralization point and pin those on multiple independent relays.*
- **high** — Index write-amplification / flooding: every drive version bump re-pushes postings; a malicious crawler can spam a hot prefix's host with high-frequency signed postings, and cover-term decoys multiply swarm joins past the 8-channel / 10-join-per-minute caps. → *Enforce per-writer posting quotas at the host keyed on writerKey (meta!writers rep/postingCount already in the design); rate-limit pushes by swarm-grant budget. Reconcile the cover-term scheme with the real swarm-bridge limits or route search through a dedicated, separately-budgeted swarm outside the per-app bridge.*

Feasibility claims that DON'T hold:
- signForApp(driveKey, posting) proves the posting was authored by the drive's owner, so 'self-submit signature proves they are the drive's per-app key' (discoveryDesign) and the writerKey is an attributable, drive-bound publisher identity. — FALSE and load-bearing. identity.js:262 signForApp derives the keypair from the CRAWLER's own seed: subSeed = hash(self._seed ++ 'pear-app-v1:' ++ driveKeyHex) (identity.js:240-247). It signs with a key that has zero cryptographic relationship to the Hyperdrive's actual signing key. A drive's key is drive.key from a fresh Corestore namespace (site-manager.js:28-32, catalog-manager.js:557), NOT identity-derived. So ANY peer can call signForApp(victimDriveKey, fakePosting) and produce a 'valid' signature claiming any driveKey. The signature proves only 'some PearBrowser identity signed this string' — it does NOT prove drive ownership, term occurrence, or honesty. The anti-Sybil claim that postings are 'attributable to a writer' survives, but the claim that self-submission authenticates the publisher, and any ranking that trusts writerKey==drive-owner, is broken. This is the single biggest factual error.
- The DHT pointer layer (mutablePut/mutableGet/immutablePut) is 'zero new networking / zero new discovery code' reusing what exists. — FALSE. grep of the entire backend finds NO mutablePut/mutableGet/immutablePut usage anywhere — it is 100% new code. HyperDHT (hyperdht 6.30.0) does expose mutablePut/mutableGet (index.js:301/354) and immutablePut (:280), so it is BUILDABLE, but the proposal mis-cites it as already-wired infrastructure. Worse, a mutable record is single-keypair, single-value, last-write-wins (persistent.js onmutableput, SEQ_REUSED/SEQ_TOO_LOW). It cannot hold 'the SET of peers/shard-cores hosting a prefix' — each host needs its own announce keypair and a client has no way to enumerate all hosts' announce keys from the DHT. The set must come from the swarm topic anyway, making the DHT pointer layer largely redundant with swarm.join. Value size is bounded to ~1 request frame (~1KB), so the <1KB claim is at the edge but OK for a single host's hint.
- swarm.join(shardTopic(prefix)) is 'the same join the catalog already does (sheets-catalog.js:139)' — zero new discovery code. — MISLEADING. sheets-catalog.js:139 joins the drive's OWN discoveryKey (a content-addressed core's topic where the host is already announcing). shardTopic = blake2b('pear-search-shard-v1'++prefix) is a SYNTHETIC rendezvous topic with no core behind it — a fundamentally different (though Hyperswarm-supported) pattern. The capability exists in hyperswarm 4.17.0, but it is new code, new semantics, and crucially the page-facing SwarmBridge caps maxChannelsPerApp:8 and maxJoinsPerMinute:10 (swarm-bridge.js:33-34). A 3-word query + K cover terms = many topic joins, blowing those caps in 1-2 queries unless the search path bypasses the bridge entirely — which contradicts the repeated 'through the existing chokepoint' claim.
- hiveRelay.seed(shardCoreKey) pins a shard with 'zero new relay code' and the relay /search route reuses existing relay infra. — PARTIALLY FALSE. hiveRelay.seed (index.js:1592) / requestSeed posts {key} and the relay re-replicates that core's discoveryKey — generic Hypercore replication CAN pin a bare Hyperbee core (one core) as well as a Hyperdrive (two cores), so durability-pinning is plausible with no change. BUT the relay's fetch path is explicitly 'HTTP fast-path for Hyperdrive content' (relay-client.js:2) serving GET /<key>/<path> with Hyperdrive file semantics. The optional GET /search?prefix=&termHash= route is brand-new server-side relay code that must run Hyperbee range scans — directly contradicting 'zero new relay code' / 'pinDriveBestEffort verbatim'. The relay only pins; it never crawls, so durability holds ONLY after a volunteer first builds the shard core.
- Anchor-proof (re-fetch hyper://driveKey@version/index.html and confirm the term occurs) makes keyword-spam non-free and is the data-layer anti-spam. — WEAK at scale. Hyperdrive does support @version checkout, so a single anchor check is mechanically possible. But: (1) it is an O(1) network drive fetch PER posting verified — a host/verifier cannot afford to anchor-check every posting on a hot prefix; the proposal admits it runs 'lazily, or on top-K only', which is exactly where a spammer can flood the long tail unchecked. (2) Anchor proves the term OCCURS, not that the page is RELEVANT — a spammer hides 10k high-value terms in invisible text in a real drive they actually publish (cheap: one Hyperdrive) and passes every anchor check. Anchor raises the cost from 'free string' to 'one published drive per spam cluster' but does NOT stop term-stuffing, which is the threat it claims to kill. Identity.verify (cited for the verifier) also does not exist in identity.js — only sign/signForApp; verification code is new.
- Multi-keyword AND via per-term scatter-gather across shards, bounded by Bloom co-term prefilters and a 600ms window, can feel instant on common queries. — UNPROVEN — and the proposal itself names this the killer risk. The fundamentals are unchanged from YaCy: a 3-word query hits 3 different prefix shards on 3 different volunteer host sets; popular terms (Zipf) have huge posting lists and hot-prefix hosts become contention/centralization points. The Bloom co-term prefilter is itself unsound at scale: a per-shard Bloom of ALL driveKeys carrying a term can be hundreds of KB for a popular term, must be shipped per query, and false positives still cross the wire. Tail latency across independent volunteer hosts within a 600ms interactive budget is exactly what no prior P2P inverted index achieved. No measurement is offered; this is the load-bearing unknown and it does not hold on the evidence.
- Query privacy: hash-only-on-wire + K=2-4 cover terms + prefix coarsening + local-first is materially better than YaCy and degrades gracefully. — OVERSTATED. termHash=blake2b(term) over a public, low-entropy term dictionary is trivially reversed by any host (rainbow table of common terms) — the proposal concedes this ('it can'). Joining shardTopic(prefix) leaks 8-12 bits of EACH query term to every host on that topic and to DHT-path observers via the announce. Cover terms collide with the existing maxChannelsPerApp:8 / 10-joins-min caps (swarm-bridge.js), so K decoys per term is not free and may be rate-blocked. Cross-query traffic analysis over a heavy user is explicitly unsolved. 'Better than plaintext YaCy' is a low bar; the honest cons section is correct that this is NOT solved and PIR is punted to v3.

### Trustweave: Federated Web-of-Curated-Indexes

**Thesis.** Search is not one engine but a federation of many small, signed, queryable Hyperbee/schema-sheets "index rooms," each owned and curated by a human or an AI agent, that the browser aggregates and ranks client-side over the user's web-of-trust. We already shipped 90% of this: the `apps` schema room (sheets-catalog.js) IS a queryable signed index with `memberkey` provenance, a `verification` enum, and `addRowAttestation`; CatalogManager.getAggregatedApps() IS the federation aggregator; HiveRelay.seed() IS the durability floor; identity.signForApp() IS the per-publisher signing; safeJmesPath() IS the privacy-preserving on-device query chokepoint. Trustweave generalizes the catalog of *apps* into a catalog of *anything* (pages, drives, datasets, agents) by adding one new schema (`resources`) and one new room type (`trust` edges), keeping the exact same load/aggregate/query/pin/sign machinery. Ranking is a pure deterministic function of the candidate set times a personalized PageRank-style trust score over signed `trust` edges — no central authority, no global DHT inverted index, no token, no pay-to-rank. The room is an index, not an authority: every row is independently re-fetchable and re-verifiable client-side. We explicitly do NOT attempt global full-text web search in v1; we attack the discovery/directory problem (intent -> hyper:// keys) where curated federation provably works (Almonit), and leave a clean seam for opt-in full-text later.

**Index.** DATA STRUCTURE: one physical schema-sheets room (Autobase + HyperDB view) per curator index = one shard. Inside, a new `resources` schema (same ajv-validated, JMESPath-queryable mechanism as the shipped `apps` schema in sheets-catalog.js:24): { title: string<=200; hyperUrl: string<=300 (hyper://<hex|z32>[/path]); driveKey: ^[0-9a-f]{64}$ (optional, for installables); kind: enum['site','drive','app','dataset','feed','room','agent']; terms: array<string<=40> maxItems 64 (lowercased, stemmed keywords/tags — the searchable field); description: string<=1000; lang: string<=8; contentHash: ^[0-9a-f]{64}$ (sha256 of the fetched index.html/manifest at crawl time, the freshness/poison anchor); crawledAt: integer; verification: enum['unverified','relay-listed','author-signed','multi-curator'] }. required: ['title','terms']; anyOf: hyperUrl|driveKey. schema-sheets auto-stamps memberkey=writer key; optional addRowAttestation row carries the curator's ed25519 sig. The room's time index + JMESPath give server-side(=on-device)-style filtering without prefix-scan abuse. KEY SCHEMAS — inside each room (HyperDB-managed; we ride schema-sheets, we do not hand-roll keys): `schema!resources`, `row!<uuid>` -> resource record, `attest!<uuid>` -> attestation. We add ONE convenience sub-database for term lookup using Hyperbee sub()/range scans only IF a single room grows large: `t!<term>!<uuid>` -> 1 (a per-room secondary index built deterministically from rows; range-scan `gte:'t!foo!', lt:'t!foo!~'` to get postings for one term WITHIN one room — never a global cross-room term core, which is fatal for Corestore). TRUST room: `trust` schema { subject: ^[0-9a-f]{64}$ (curator pubkey); weight: integer -100..100; tags: array<string<=40>; revoked: boolean }. DHT: a 32-byte topic = blake2b('trustweave:directory:v1') for room announce; relay also seeds keys it pins. SHARDING: by curator, not by term — natural, attributable, ban-able. No global term-sharded DHT index (the non-negotiable). WHO STORES: the curator's device + HiveRelay replicas (replicas:3) + every subscriber who loaded the room (each subscriber is a seeder — the YaCy-good property without the YaCy-bad ranking). UPDATE/FRESHNESS: Autobase append for new rows; createDiffStream over the room's HyperDB version gives incremental "what changed since I last queried" so a subscriber only re-ranks deltas; relay re-pins on the discoveryKey after edits (autobeeDiscoveryKey-style hook already exists). Stale-poison defense: contentHash + crawledAt let the client detect a row pointing at content that no longer matches, and a PoR challenge ("still hold room X at length>=L?") docks a relay's reputation on failure.

**Ranking / anti-sybil.** SCORE(row) = termMatch(row, query) * trust(row.curator) * verifyBonus(row.verification) * coListBonus(row.hyperUrl). PURE + DETERMINISTIC (modeled on autobee-catalog-apply.cjs: no Date.now anywhere in the scorer; computed only after a bounded collection window over the fully-assembled candidate set; ties broken by (contentHash, curatorPubKey) so orderings never flicker run-to-run). termMatch = count of query terms present in row.terms/title (exact + prefix), normalized — NOT a publisher-controlled page feature, so it is unspoofable by the content owner; the curator chose the terms and is accountable for them. trust(curator) = personalized PageRank seeded from the querying user's own signed TrustRoom edges, propagated over the union of all loaded TrustRooms (weight-damped, edge cap per source to bound influence). A curator the user follows directly scores highest; a curator two hops out scores less; an unknown curator scores at a small floor only if relay-listed. coListBonus rewards a hyper:// key that independent, mutually-trusted curators ALL listed — convergent independent curation is the single strongest honest-vs-spam signal and is cheap to compute from the candidate set. SYBIL/SPAM RESISTANCE (layered, all on shipped primitives): (1) every row signed (memberkey + optional attestation) — anonymous spam is impossible; (2) per-writer-core isolation — a bad curator is dropped wholesale by removing their trust edge, instantly zeroing every row they ever wrote (read-time set-union, no rewrite needed); (3) trust-graph rank means Sybils, being poorly connected to the honest graph, get ~floor score regardless of how many rooms/rows they spam (SybilLimit/MeritRank property); (4) coListBonus requires INDEPENDENT trusted curators, which Sybils cannot fake without first earning honest trust edges; (5) verification enum gates floor eligibility (unverified curators never out-rank a followed one); (6) bounded fan-out + per-source edge cap stop a Sybil cluster from dominating the candidate set. No score is derivable from content the publisher controls; all of it derives from who-vouches-for-whom, which is signed and costly to forge.

**Discovery.** The bootstrap chicken-and-egg (keyless content has no name to search for, and rooms themselves are keyless): solved by treating ROOMS AS RESOURCES and layering three discovery tiers. TIER 0 — baked-in: ship a canonical bootstrap set in the build — the relay-published `directory` room z32 (the still-open operator TBD, identical to the catalog's pending canonical-relay question) plus a few default-trusted founding curator rooms. The directory room is itself a ResourceRoom whose rows have kind='room' and describe other ResourceRooms (title, link, curator pubkey, topic tags, coverage stats). Loading the directory is the same loadCatalogSheets call as any room. TIER 1 — DHT announce: every room owner announces under topic = blake2b('trustweave:directory:v1'); a peer doing Hyperswarm lookup on that topic finds live room-serving peers and can pull their self-describing room manifest, so new rooms are discoverable without waiting for a relay to list them (the relay just makes it durable/fast). TIER 2 — social transitive: when you follow a curator, you also import their `resources` rows of kind='room' and their TrustRoom edges, so trusted curators recommend each other's indexes — discovery rides the trust graph (a trusted curator's "I index these other good rooms" is high-signal). NAMING/Zooko: human-readable aliases live as resource rows — a `resources` row IS a {human title/terms -> 32-byte key} binding, signed and curator-attributed, so "find the keet site" resolves to keet's hyper:// key via whichever curators bound that name; conflicting bindings are disambiguated by trust+coList, not by a global registrar. Bootstrapping content into the index: (a) curators hand-add rows in the UI; (b) CuratorAgents crawl a seed set of known drives (starting from the existing app catalogue's driveKeys) and auto-emit signed rows; (c) any drive owner can self-list by publishing their own one-room ResourceRoom and submitting it to the directory (relay-listed verification tier on acceptance).

**Privacy.** QUERY PRIVACY by construction: the default and only v1 path is LOCAL-FIRST. The browser replicates each subscribed ResourceRoom in full (it is small — curated, not the whole web) and runs the JMESPath query ON DEVICE (catalog-manager.js querySheetsCatalog / sheets-catalog.js listApps). The query string never leaves the machine — zero query leakage, structurally, with no PIR/ORAM needed. This is the property YaCy lost by shipping plaintext queries to a remote Solr; we never make that call. METADATA LEAKAGE controls: (a) FIX THE EXISTING LEAK — hyper-proxy.js:678-694 currently races a relay HTTP fetch against P2P for nearly every request, leaking which hyper:// keys you open to the default-on relay. Make that race P2P-FIRST: only fall to the relay after a P2P timeout, and never for a key the user marked private. Without this, click-through after a private query still leaks the destination. (b) Subscribing to a room joins its discoveryKey on the swarm, which reveals interest in that room (not the query) to the room's swarm peers — acceptable, same as following a public feed; users who want to hide even that can replicate via a relay proxy. (c) TrustRooms are public by design (trust must federate to be computable), so who you follow is visible — this is a deliberate trade; a private-trust mode (encrypted TrustRoom, link not shared, used only locally for ranking) is the documented opt-in. (d) Result click-through uses the hyper:// proxy; with the P2P-first fix the destination is fetched over the swarm, not announced to the relay. OPT-IN RELAY FAST-PATH (deferred, never default): a relay can host a query endpoint over hashed terms for cold-start/long-tail, rate-limited and clearly flagged as semi-trusted leakage — explicitly off until trust-graph coverage is real. No raw user text is ever concatenated into a JMESPath (safeJmesPath chokepoint); result size is capped (MAX_SHEETS_ROWS).

**Incentives.** DURABILITY FLOOR lives on relays, NOT volunteers — this is the structural fix for the free-rider death (YaCy) and single-operator death (ipfs-search): a curator's room is pinned by HiveRelay (replicas:3) the moment it is published, so it survives the curator and any single relay going offline. WHY PEERS INDEX/CURATE: curation is self-interested and attributable — a curator builds a followed, reputation-bearing identity (their pubkey accrues trust edges); good curation directly raises their rooms' rank for everyone who trusts them, which is the reward. AI CuratorAgents make this scale: an agent operator runs a crawler-curator because their room becoming a default-trusted, high-coverage index is valuable standing. WHY PEERS SERVE/STORE: every subscriber who loads a room automatically re-seeds it (Hyperswarm replication is symmetric), so popular rooms get more replicas for free — the good half of YaCy's volunteer model, decoupled from ranking so it can't be gamed. WHY RELAYS PIN: relays already run for the browser's content-availability backbone; indexing rooms is the same seed() path, marginal cost ~zero, and a relay that pins broadly becomes the cold-start `directory` source new users bootstrap from (reputational pull). INCENTIVE GUARDRAILS (non-negotiable, learned from Presearch/NFT-node corruption): rewards, if any, attach ONLY to durability/seeding/uptime — measured by PoR-style freshness challenges (relay reputation rows already exist) — and NEVER to rank. Any credit is non-transferable, expiring, redeemed only as swarm-grant standing / higher rate limits (reuse the per-app swarm grants + trust tiers + rate limits already shipped) — never a sellable token, never pay-to-rank, never NFT-gated nodes. DURABILITY is auditable: failed PoR challenges dock a relay's reputation and demote it in the directory; clients re-verify every relay claim so a defecting relay degrades availability but cannot forge or censor.

**First slice.** Ship the discovery half on top of the existing app-store with the SMALLEST possible delta: (1) Add a `resources` schema to schema-sheets alongside the existing `apps` schema (sheets-catalog.js — copy the APPS_SCHEMA block, add terms[]/hyperUrl/kind/contentHash). (2) Reuse loadCatalogSheets/querySheetsCatalog verbatim to load and on-device-query a handful of ResourceRooms (the dev catalogue already proves the create->seed->pin->register->query loop end to end, index.js:1540-1555). (3) Add a `trust` schema + a 'Follow curator' button in the UI that appends ONE signed trust edge (identity.signForApp). (4) Implement a v1 TrustRanker that is just two tiers — rows from directly-followed curators rank above rows from the baked-in default set above everything else — plus the coList bonus and the (contentHash,curatorPubKey) tie-break (full personalized PageRank is v2; the pure-function scaffold ships now). (5) Wire a single relay-pinned `directory` ResourceRoom as the baked-in bootstrap (one z32, the same TBD operator decision as the catalogue). NO crawler, NO full-text, NO DHT beacon, NO PoR audit in slice 1 — those are v2. Deliverable: type intent, get a ranked list of hyper:// keys from curators you trust, fully on-device, fully signed, durable via the existing pin path. This is a 'federated bookmarks/directory you can search and rank by trust' — provably useful, and every later phase is additive.

**Killer risk.** Curator cold-start coverage. The entire model is a directory that is only as good as what humans/agents have chosen to index — and on day one almost nothing is indexed. Unlike a crawler-based engine, Trustweave has no answer for a query whose target no trusted curator has listed; it returns empty where Google returns a million results, and users churn on the first miss. The trust-graph ranking, the privacy, the durability, the Sybil-resistance are all genuinely solved-or-solvable, but they rank an empty shelf until curation reaches critical mass. Everything therefore hinges on bootstrapping coverage fast: a small set of high-quality default-trusted CuratorAgents that auto-crawl the known hyper:// universe (seeded from the existing app catalogue driveKeys + the relay's pin registry) and emit signed `resources` rows must exist and run before users do — and if those few founding agents become the de-facto authority, we have quietly rebuilt the centralized crawler we set out to avoid, just wearing a federation costume. The mitigation (many independent attributable curators + transitive trust discovery) is plausible but unproven, and it is a social/operational bootstrap problem, not a technical one we can fully engineer away.

Top threats:

An Autobase room is N writer cores + view cores, not one drive. While the curator is online the relay can passively replicate cores requested over the swarm, but there is no Autobase-aware logic to enumerate/keep-alive all writer cores or follow addWriter events, and the HTTP fast-path that makes content reachable when the publisher is OFFLINE only serves Hyperdrives. The bonus amplifies whatever the seed set says, including coordinated early curators.

### HiveSearch: Relay-Hosted Sharded Inverted Index over Schema-Sheets, Client-Verified and Personally Re-Ranked

**Thesis.** Treat full-text search as a strict, additive extension of the index-room machinery we already shipped (IndexRoomClient + the relay index sidecar's four schema-sheets schemas + hiveRelay.seed pinning + capabilitySig/verifyCapabilityDoc + per-app signing + addRowAttestation). HiveRelay and opt-in volunteer index nodes run crawlers that build a *sharded Hyperbee inverted index* (one physical Hyperbee per term-prefix shard, never one core per term) plus a signed doc-manifest room; clients get fast first-paint by querying a relay's HTTP `/index/search` fast-path, but the relay is an index, not an authority: every returned posting carries the publisher's per-app Ed25519 signature and an anchor/shard-membership proof that the client re-verifies P2P before it counts, then the client re-ranks the verified candidate set locally with a deterministic, pure ranking function over BM25 + a personal web-of-trust derived from existing identity/grant edges. Relays are swappable because the shard format is content-addressed and re-hostable by key; a captured relay can degrade availability or hide results, but cannot forge a ranked result a client will accept. The hard, honest weakness — query privacy against the relay fast-path, and trust-graph cold-start — is bounded by making local-first the default posture and the relay path explicitly opt-in and decoy-padded.

**Index.** Two cooperating structures. (A) The INVERTED INDEX is a fixed fan-out of physical Hyperbees — 256 shards selected by `shardId = sha256(term)[0:1]` (2 hex; bump to 4096/3-hex past ~10M docs). Each shard is ONE Hyperbee (corestore cannot survive one-core-per-term — that is the fatal anti-pattern we explicitly avoid). Primary key inside a shard: `t!<termHash16>!<scoreInv32be>!<docId16>` → value `{driveKey,path,tf,positions?}`. termHash16 = first 16 hex of sha256(term) (collision-tolerant; doc-manifest disambiguates). scoreInv32be = big-endian (0xFFFFFFFF − floor(tfScore)) so a plain ascending range scan with `limit:k` yields the top-k highest-scoring docs for a term in ONE read — no full-list materialization (this is the bound that keeps us out of YaCy's scatter-gather hole). Secondary sub-database in the same bee: `df!<termHash16>` → document-frequency counter for IDF, and `meta!docCount`/`meta!epoch`. (B) The DOC-MANIFEST + INDEX-SHARD ROOM is a schema-sheets room (same room family as today's index room) with two new schemas. `doc-manifest`: {docId(required,16hex of sha256(driveKey++path)), driveKey(64hex,required), path, title, lang, length(int), termCount(int), driveVersion(int), publisherPubkey(64hex), sig(128hex over the canonical doc record), crawledAt(int), shardEpoch(int), verification enum}; required [docId,driveKey,shardEpoch]; additionalProperties:true (forward-compat, matching the §8 decision for manifest-class schemas). `index-shard`: {shardId(2-4 hex,required), shardKey(64hex Hyperbee key,required), termRangeLo, termRangeHi, docCount(int), treeHash(64hex — the shard core's signed tree hash), treeLength(int — core length the treeHash commits to), epoch(int,required), crawlerPubkey(64hex), sig(128hex)}; required [shardId,shardKey,epoch]; additionalProperties:false (trust-bearing). WHO STORES IT: the crawler/relay writes both; HiveRelay pins both via hiveRelay.seed (replicas:3). Clients blind-replicate read-only. UPDATE/FRESHNESS: each crawl epoch the crawler diffs changed drives, appends new postings to the live shard bees (append-only; supersession handled at read time by docId + driveVersion — newest driveVersion wins, mirroring the version-wins dedup in getAggregatedApps), updates the `df!` counters, then writes a fresh `index-shard` row with a bumped epoch + new treeHash@treeLength and bumps `searchShardEpoch` in the capability doc. Because shards are append-only Hypercores, a stale relay is detectable: client compares the served treeLength against the `index-shard` row's treeLength and against the capability doc's epoch; a regression = stale-snapshot signal that docks the relay's freshness reputation. HyperDHT holds NOTHING durable here — at most a tiny signed `searchIndexRoom→z32` hint with the usual 48h TTL; postings never live in the DHT.

**Ranking / anti-sybil.** Ranking is a PURE, deterministic function computed client-side AFTER a bounded collection window over the VERIFIED candidate set only (so a relay that returns extra junk cannot move ordering, and the order never flickers between runs — the autobee no-wall-clock discipline applied to search). finalScore(doc) = BM25(query,doc) × trustWeight(publisher) × freshness(crawledAt). BM25 uses tf from the posting + df/docCount from the shard `df!`/`meta!` counters + length/termCount from the doc-manifest — all independently re-fetchable, none publisher-asserted in a gameable free-text field. trustWeight comes from a bounded personalized rank over a STATIC edge set the user already owns: edges = swarm-grants (granted app ⇒ trusted publisher, weighted by trust tier), contacts, and publishers whose catalogue rows the user has installed/bookmarked, seeded with a small bootstrap set (relay-listed + default-trusted publishers in the relay-directory). We run a few iterations of MeritRank/personalized-PageRank from the user's own node so trust is RELATIVE to the user, not global — no global authority, and a Sybil cluster poorly connected to the user's honest edges gets ~0 weight regardless of how many fake docs it published. SYBIL/SPAM RESISTANCE, layered: (1) every doc record is signed by a per-app derived key bound to the driveKey it indexes — you can only publish postings for content you hold the key to, so keyword-stuffing a competitor's page is impossible. (2) Per-crawler-core isolation: postings from a crawler are attributable to its `index-shard` row's crawlerPubkey; a bad crawler's whole shard set can be dropped at read-time set-union (the same "drop a writer wholesale" move schema-sheets gives us). (3) trustWeight gates ranking, not write access, so an open index stays open but spam sinks to ~0. (4) verification enum: unverified (default) < relay-listed (relay attested it crawled the doc) < author-signed (publisher attestation via addRowAttestation) — clients can hard-filter to ≥relay-listed. (5) page-feature signals (title match boosts etc.) are capped and never dominate trust, because publisher-controlled fields are exactly what YaCy got gamed on. No pay-to-rank: there is no field, stake, or token a publisher can spend to raise finalScore.

**Discovery.** Two layers, because the brief's two engines must not be conflated. DISCOVERY (intent→key) is shippable now and is the human-name→key naming layer: the doc-manifest room's `title`/`name`/`driveKey` rows ARE a signed directory — a query that matches a title resolves intent to a hyper:// key with provenance, exactly the Almonit 'curated signed directory, client-side search' pattern that historically worked, but federated and signed instead of one operator's JSON. New content BOOTSTRAPS into the index three ways, all keyless-friendly: (1) PUBLISH-TIME SELF-ANNOUNCE — when a user publishes a site/app (the existing publish path that already calls hiveRelay.seed), we additively submit the driveKey to the relay's crawl frontier (one app-suggestions-style signed row), so a brand-new key with no human name is discovered the moment it is pinned; the crawler then derives its searchable terms from the content itself (title, headings, body) — the human-readable names come from the CONTENT, not from a pre-existing alias. (2) LINK-GRAPH CRAWL — the crawler extracts in-page hyper://<key> anchors from already-known pages and adds them to the frontier, so content reachable from indexed content is found transitively without any registry. (3) CATALOGUE/INDEX-ROOM UNION — every driveKey already in any loaded catalogue room or the relay index room's app-manifest is a frontier seed for free. NAMING/ALIASING: we do NOT build a global human-name→key registry (that recreates DNS/an authority). Instead, human-meaningful naming is emergent and per-trust-context: a name resolves to the highest-trust-weighted doc-manifest row whose title matches, so 'pearmail' resolves to whichever 'pearmail' your trust graph vouches for — Zooko's triangle handled by making the 'human-meaningful' corner relative to the user's trust set rather than globally unique. Cold-start discoverability of a key nobody links to yet is handled solely by self-announce at publish time.

**Privacy.** This is the honest soft spot and the design treats it as a posture, not a solved problem. DEFAULT POSTURE = LOCAL-FIRST: the client replicates only the shard Hyperbees for its query terms + the doc-manifest room and runs term scans + intersection on-device, leaking ZERO query text to any relay (the same on-device JMESPath posture the catalogue already has). The cost is replicating a shard (one of 256) per distinct query term — bounded, cacheable across queries, and warmed in the background for common terms. The metadata leak that remains even local-first is which shard discoveryKeys you swarm-join; we blunt this by (a) joining a fixed rotating cover-set of shards rather than exactly the query shards, and (b) keeping a hot local cache so repeated/related queries touch no new shard. OPT-IN FAST PATH: `/index/search` gives one-round-trip first-paint but sends (hashed) terms to a semi-trusted relay — this is OFF by default, surfaced as an explicit "use relay for faster search (shares your query with <relay>)" toggle, rate-limited, and mitigated three ways: terms are sent as sha256(term) salted-per-epoch (the relay sees term hashes, not plaintext, and can't trivially reverse rare terms but CAN dictionary-attack common ones — stated plainly), the client pads with k decoy term-hashes per real term so the relay sees a set it can't disambiguate, and the client rotates which relay it asks. A future hardening lane (NOT v1) is hashed-bucket PIR / oblivious shard fetch; we deliberately do not block v1 on it. METADATA: relay logs could correlate (IP, time, shard set); the relay-vs-P2P fetch race in hyper-proxy (_doHybridFetch, :678-694) currently leaks nearly every fetch to the default-on relay — we make the search fetch path P2P-FIRST so verification re-fetches don't re-leak the click. Result clicks open hyper:// through the normal proxy, which has the same relay-leak property the rest of the browser has — search introduces no new leak there. Net: v1 is genuinely private in local mode, honestly-not-private in opt-in relay mode, and we ship the toggle so the user chooses.

**Incentives.** Durability does NOT depend on volunteer goodwill — that is the lesson from YaCy (volunteer churn) and ipfs-search (single operator). The durability FLOOR lives on HiveRelay: every shard Hyperbee + doc-manifest room is pinned via hiveRelay.seed(replicas:3), so the index survives any individual crawler going offline, with zero new relay code. Volunteer "crawler peers" and extra index nodes are ADDITIVE coverage/freshness, not the floor — if they vanish, the relay-pinned shards still answer queries (just less fresh). WHY peers crawl/index/serve: (1) self-interest — a publisher runs a crawler to get its own content indexed faster and attested (author-signed), and an enthusiast runs one to make a topic area searchable for their own community room. (2) reputation, not money — a crawler's freshness and shard-availability are measured by PoR-style audits (see below) and recorded as relay-directory/verification reputation; high-reputation crawlers' shards are preferred in the client's bootstrap set, which is the only "reward" and it is non-transferable. (3) NO TOKEN, NO PAY-TO-RANK, NO NFT-GATED NODES — any incentive credit rewards DURABILITY/SEEDING (kept a shard available at the claimed epoch), never rank, and is a non-transferable, expiring "credit" redeemed only as swarm-grant standing / higher rate-limits, never a sellable asset (this directly avoids Presearch keyword-staking and NFT recentralization). DURABILITY AUDIT: freshness/availability is a verifiable challenge — a verifier peer (already a supported room writer) periodically asks "still hold shard X at treeLength≥L, crawled within T?" and the holder must answer with a Hyperbee proof at that length; a failed audit docks reputation and demotes that holder in client bootstrap. Stale-snapshot serving (relay returns epoch N when capability doc says N+2) is the same audit, client-side, every query.

**First slice.** Ship DISCOVERY-as-search on top of what already exists, with zero crawler. Concretely: (1) Add two schemas to the relay index room family — `doc-manifest` and `index-shard` — but for the first slice populate `doc-manifest` ONLY from data we already have: one row per app-manifest/apps row already in the index/catalogue rooms (driveKey, title=name, terms tokenized from name+description+categories), signed by the relay writer. No HTML crawl yet. (2) Extend backend/index-room-client.js with `listDocs(jmespath)` (mirrors the existing listApps/listVerifications readers, reuses safeJmesPath + MAX_SHEETS_ROWS) and a tiny client-side scorer: tokenize the user query, JMESPath-filter doc-manifest rows by term, and rank with BM25-lite × trustWeight where trustWeight is just {3:granted-app, 2:relay-listed, 1:unverified} from swarm-grants + the verification enum (full MeritRank deferred). (3) Re-verify each returned row's `capabilitySig`/attestation exactly as listRelayDirectory already does, dropping the rest. (4) Surface it in the Apps/Explore search box as 'search the federated index' returning ranked hyper:// results with provenance, merged through getAggregatedApps. This delivers real intent→key search, exercises the full verify-then-rank pipeline, reuses the IndexRoomClient + pin path + identity primitives unchanged, and pins the doc-manifest room via the existing pinDriveBestEffort line — without writing a crawler, a sharded inverted index, or the relay /index/search route. The inverted-index shards, the crawler peer, the HTTP fast-path, and MeritRank are the next slices, each independently shippable behind a flag.

**Killer risk.** Trust-graph bootstrap collapsing into relay centralization. The whole 'index not authority' claim rests on clients re-ranking with a PERSONAL trust graph — but a new user has no edges, so in practice ranking falls back to the relay-listed/default-trusted bootstrap set, and if 95% of users never build personal edges, the default relay's blessing effectively IS the ranking authority. At that point a captured or biased relay shapes what everyone sees, re-verification only proves authenticity (not relevance or completeness), and we have rebuilt a central search authority wearing a P2P costume. This is more likely to kill the approach than any scaling or privacy issue, because it fails silently — everything still works, results still verify, nobody notices the authority crept back in. Mitigation must be deliberate and measured: seed trust from real owned signals (every install/grant/bookmark/contact auto-adds an edge so the graph grows from normal use without asking), make the bootstrap set explicitly multi-relay and user-swappable from day one, show ranking provenance ('ranked by your trust graph' vs 'ranked by default relay') so the centralization is visible, and treat 'fraction of users on a non-default trust graph' as the headline health metric for the whole project.

The verify-then-rank cryptographic core and the sharded-Hyperbee inverted index are sound; the proposal's fatal flaw is putting the per-document manifest in the one primitive (Autobase) that cannot shard..**

Showstoppers:
- Architectural mismatch: storing one row per crawled document in an Autobase-backed schema-sheets room. The actual list() implementation (sheets-bundle.cjs:46295) does find().toArray() + per-row JMESPath in JS with no honored limit and only a rows-by-schema-time index — i.e. a full in-memory table scan per term query. Autobase is a linearized multi-writer oplog with no horizontal partitioning; it cannot scale to a millions-of-docs search corpus. The doc-manifest room must NOT be schema-sheets; it must be a docId-sharded Hyperbee like the inverted index. Until this is redesigned the corpus-scale claims are false.
- The MAX_SHEETS_ROWS cap the proposal repeatedly relies on does not exist: it is imported in index-room-client.js but never exported from sheets-catalog.js, so it is undefined and bounds nothing today. Every 'limit-capped / bounded' claim about the manifest-room read path is currently untrue. Must be fixed (and made a real DB-level limit, not a post-hoc slice) before any search read path ships.

Top threats:
- **critical** — Manifest-room full-scan blowup: list() does toArray()+JMESPath in JS with no working limit, so every client-side term query over the doc-manifest room loads the entire corpus metadata into memory. This is the YaCy scatter-gather hole, relocated to the Autobase manifest room. → *Do NOT store per-document rows in an Autobase/schema-sheets room. The manifest must be a sharded Hyperbee keyed by docId (like the inverted index), not an Autobase view. Reserve the schema-sheets room ONLY for the small, slowly-changing index-shard descriptor set (hundreds of rows) and relay/verification metadata. Term lookups must hit the inverted-index bees exclusively; the manifest is fetched by docId point-get, never scanned by term. Also fix the MAX_SHEETS_ROWS export bug before any of this ships.*
- **high** — Crawler-asserted term content: for the vast majority of docs, postings are written by the crawler (verification=relay-listed), not author-signed. The per-app signature only protects attribution, so a malicious or captured crawler controls the term->doc mapping for everything it indexes and can shadow-ban (omit) or mis-tag at will, undetectably. → *Make completeness auditable, not just authenticity: publish a per-epoch signed bloom/MPHF commitment of the full docId set per shard so omission is detectable by sampling. Strongly incentivize author-signed self-indexing at publish time (the self-announce path already exists) so the high-value corpus is publisher-asserted, and let users hard-filter to >=author-signed for sensitive queries. Treat crawler-only postings as a lower trust tier in ranking by default.*
- **high** — Relay-as-authority via trust-graph cold start (author's own killerRisk). New users have no edges, ranking falls back to the relay-blessed bootstrap set, and silent recentralization results — everything verifies, nobody notices the authority returned. → *Ship multi-relay bootstrap and the 'ranked by your trust graph vs default relay' provenance badge from day one; auto-add a trust edge on every install/grant/bookmark/contact so the graph grows from normal use; and treat 'fraction of users on a non-default trust graph' as the project's headline health metric (as the author proposes). This is mitigation, not a fix — the failure mode is inherent to any P2P search that needs a relevance prior.*
- **med** — Query privacy on the opt-in fast path: salted sha256(term) hashes are trivially dictionary-attackable for common terms; per-epoch salt is broadcast so the relay computes the same dictionary. Decoy padding raises the guessing cost only linearly and is defeated by frequency analysis across queries from the same client. → *Keep the fast path strictly opt-in and off by default (as designed), label it honestly. Do not rely on hash+decoy for real privacy. The only real fix is the deferred PIR/oblivious-fetch lane; until then, privacy-sensitive users must use local-first and eat the cost. Acceptable IF the UI never lets a user believe the fast path is private.*

Feasibility claims that DON'T hold:
- The doc-manifest + index-shard room is the SAME schema-sheets (Autobase-backed) family as the index room, holding ONE signed row per crawled document, queried client-side by term with the existing list/JMESPath path. — This is the load-bearing failure. Confirmed in backend/sheets-bundle.cjs:46295 that SchemaSheets.list() does db.find(...).toArray() — it materializes the ENTIRE schema's row set into a JS array IN MEMORY, then runs JMESPath per-row. There is no DB-level limit (the limit opt index-room-client passes is dropped — it is not in _opts) and the only index is rows-by-schema-time, so a term query is a FULL TABLE SCAN in JS over every doc row. 'One row per crawled document' in an Autobase view means: (a) every reader replays/holds the oplog to materialize the view, (b) every listDocs loads all docs into memory. At 1M+ docs this is multi-GB in memory per query and re-introduces exactly the scatter-gather blowup the author claims to avoid — just moved from the shards (which are fine) to the manifest room (which is not). Autobase is a multi-writer LINEARIZED OP LOG, not a sharded scalable store; it has no horizontal partitioning. A search corpus is the wrong shape for it.
- MAX_SHEETS_ROWS + safeJmesPath bound the doc-manifest reads (reused unchanged from the existing client). — MAX_SHEETS_ROWS is imported in backend/index-room-client.js:17 from ./sheets-catalog but is NOT exported by sheets-catalog.js (export list is SheetsCatalog, APPS_SCHEMA, rowToApp, decodeSheetsLink, safeJmesPath). It resolves to undefined: { limit: undefined } and .slice(0, undefined) impose NO bound. The cap the proposal repeatedly leans on does not exist in the current code — it is a latent bug today and would silently fail to bound a million-row search corpus. safeJmesPath does exist and is real (512-char cap, regex allowlist, backtick ban), but it sanitizes query syntax, not result-set size.
- Local-first query leaks zero query text: client replicates only the shard bees for its terms + the doc-manifest room and runs term scans on-device. — Two leaks the proposal under-rates. (1) Replicating a shard means JOINING that shard's discoveryKey topic on Hyperswarm/HyperDHT — which shard set you swarm-join is observable to the DHT and to anyone seeding those shards, and with 256 shards the shard-set fingerprints your query terms fairly well. The author admits this and offers cover-sets, but cover-sets fight the latency/cache goal. (2) More damaging: local-first requires replicating the doc-manifest ROOM, which per the list() implementation means pulling enough of the Autobase view to answer — and because there is no term index in that room, on-device term scanning requires materializing the manifest rows (toArray) locally, i.e. downloading a large fraction of the corpus metadata. 'Zero query leakage' is true for the relay, but the cost is downloading vastly more than the answer, which is the opposite of the 'one shard per term, bounded' claim. The two scaling stories (shards bounded vs manifest room full-scan) are in tension.

### Constellation — Social/Personal-First P2P Search

**Thesis.** Every user opt-in publishes a signed, replicable "personal index" — a Hyperbee of the pages and apps they actually browse, bookmark, and explicitly endorse, built directly on the existing UserData/bookmarks/history layer. Search is not a query to a global index; it is a fan-out over the personal indexes of people you already trust (your existing Contacts social graph, keyed by root pubkey), ranked by social distance, then expanded outward hop-by-hop through your contacts' contacts only as far as you need results. This inverts the YaCy failure: there is no global term-sharded DHT index to keep alive, gameable, or leak to. Cold-start is solved by your own data (you can always search what you've browsed). Sybil resistance is structural — a Sybil node is invisible until a real person in your graph endorses it, and even then it is bounded to one edge. Privacy is structural — you replicate a friend's small index and run JMESPath locally; the query never leaves your device. The whole thing is the Almonit "client-side search over a curated signed set" pattern, except the curated set is your social graph and every primitive it needs (Contacts, signed per-app keys, schema-sheets rooms, relay pinning, the verify-each-row trust pattern) already ships in this repo. Its honest weakness: coverage is bounded by your social graph's collective browsing — it is a discovery and rediscovery engine and a personalized web index, not a complete crawl of the unknown web, and it has a real filter-bubble and graph-cold-start problem for a brand-new user with zero contacts.

**Index.** No global term-sharded DHT inverted index exists. The index is FEDERATED: one PersonalIndex Hyperbee per user, sharded implicitly by author (your index, my index). Inside each PersonalIndex Hyperbee (utf-8 keys, json values), TWO sub-spaces co-located in one core (never one core per term — that is fatal for Corestore): \n\n(A) POSTINGS sub-space, the inverted index, keyed for range-scan retrieval: \n  `t!<term>!<invScore>!<docId>` -> { docId, driveKey, path, titleHit:bool, field:'title'|'body', tf:int, sig:hex, endorser:pubkey, publishedAt:int }\n  where <term> is a lowercased, stopword-stripped token; <invScore> is String(Number.MAX_SAFE_INTEGER - localTermScore).padStart(18,'0') so a plain forward range scan `gte:'t!react!', lt:'t!react!~'` returns this author's best matches for `react` FIRST, in O(results) — exactly the inverted-timestamp trick already used in user-data.js for history ordering. Multi-term AND is a bounded client-side intersection of these small per-author candidate lists (each capped, e.g. top-50/term/author), never a federation-wide intersection — this is the deliberate answer to the #4 multi-term scaling tax: intersect only small pre-filtered per-author sets. \n\n(B) DOCS sub-space, the forward index / dedup + render source: \n  `d!<docId>` -> { docId, driveKey, path, title, terms:[...], endorsedAt, endorseSig, visibility:'shared'|'endorsed'|'private', publishedAt }\n  docId = first 16 bytes hex of sha256(driveKey + '\\0' + path) — stable, dedup-friendly, mirrors the catalog's uuid-as-stable-id discipline. \n\n(C) META: `meta!version`->2, `meta!owner`-><rootPubkey>, `meta!updatedAt`. \n\nWHO STORES IT: the author stores their own index; HiveRelay pins it (replicas:3) for offline availability; a querier replicates read-only the subset of indexes in their trust frontier. FRESHNESS/UPDATE: PersonalIndexer appends on each new endorsement; the IndexPointer DHT mutable record bumps a seq each publish; queriers call base/core update() on replicate (newly-appended postings stream in via Hyperbee's append-only log). Incremental sync across query sessions reuses Hyperbee createDiffStream so a re-query only pulls deltas. There is NO central index to shard or keep alive — durability is per-author + relay pin, the exact gap YaCy and ipfs-search never closed.

**Ranking / anti-sybil.** Ranking is a PURE function (no wall-clock in the score, same discipline as autobee-catalog-apply.cjs) computed once after a bounded collection window over the assembled candidate set + a static snapshot of the trust frontier. Score(doc) = w1*socialProximity + w2*endorserBreadth + w3*tierWeight + w4*termMatch + w5*freshness, where: socialProximity = 1/(hop+1) (hop-1 friend beats hop-2 friend-of-friend); endorserBreadth = log(1 + distinct frontier endorsers of this doc) (the killer signal — 'this URL is endorsed by 5 of your friends' is near-unfakeable); tierWeight from the existing swarm-grants A/B/C tiers of each endorser; termMatch from the per-posting record (title hit > body hit, all-terms-present phrase bonus, tf); freshness = exp(-age/halflife) using publishedAt vs a query-time constant passed in (kept OUT of the per-doc fold so two runs over the same frontier produce identical order). SYBIL/SPAM RESISTANCE is structural and is the whole point: (1) An attacker's fake index is INVISIBLE — it is never in anyone's trust frontier until a real person adds them as a Contact (a signed QR/invite ceremony, not a free action). (2) Even after one honest edge, a Sybil contributes at most one endorser to endorserBreadth and sits at hop>=1 with whatever low tier the single honest contact assigned — it cannot manufacture breadth because breadth counts DISTINCT frontier pubkeys and the frontier is your real graph (this is the SybilLimit insight: attack edges, not attack nodes, bound influence). (3) Every posting is signature-verified against an endorser pubkey that MUST be a member of the current frontier, re-checked client-side at query time (the IndexRoomClient 'verify each row, drop the rest' pattern) — a relay serving a friend's index cannot inject postings signed by anyone outside your graph. (4) A misbehaving contact is dropped wholesale (remove the Contact -> their entire index leaves your frontier, the per-writer-core isolation property). Page-controlled features (meta tags, keyword stuffing) carry ZERO ranking weight — only signed social endorsements and term presence do, which is exactly what YaCy's gameable local page-feature ranking lacked.

**Discovery.** This is where 'content addressed by opaque keys' is actually solved, because the social graph IS the naming layer (Zooko: the key stays secure+decentralized; HUMAN-MEANING is supplied by a person you trust who endorsed it under a title). KEYLESS CONTENT GETS A NAME the moment a real user bookmarks/endorses it: the PersonalIndexer captures driveKey+path AND the human title from the page AND the endorser's identity, so 'that hyper key' becomes 'the P2P blog Maya endorsed about Hypercore' in your index. NAMING/BOOTSTRAP: (a) You can always search your own browsed/bookmarked content from day one (cold-start via own data — no graph needed). (b) New keys enter the searchable universe ONLY through a human act of endorsement, which is also the Sybil gate — discovery and trust are the same edge. (c) FINDING WHO TO ASK: contact-pubkey -> index-key resolution via the DHT IndexPointer (schema below) plus contact-record fallback; the social graph itself is bootstrapped by the EXISTING pear://contact?pk=..&sig=.. signed QR invite ceremony (backend/contacts.js parseInviteURL) — no new discovery mechanism needed. (d) GRAPH COLD-START for a brand-new user with zero contacts: a default-trusted 'starter index' published by the relay as a schema-sheets index room (loaded via IndexRoomClient verbatim, every row re-verified) seeds hop-0 so search returns SOMETHING on install; the user then replaces it organically as they add contacts. The starter index is explicitly framed as a removable bootstrap, not an authority, to avoid ossifying into a central directory (the open #8 filter-bubble risk). Human-name->key aliasing is therefore emergent (titles carried on signed postings) rather than a separate petname system to build.

**Privacy.** Local-first by construction, the strongest posture available. THE QUERY NEVER LEAVES THE DEVICE in the common path: to search a friend's index you REPLICATE their PersonalIndex Hyperbee (you pull blocks for the term! prefixes you scan) and run the range scan + intersection ON YOUR DEVICE. The friend's relay/peer sees Hyperbee block requests, not your query string. METADATA LEAKAGE — the honest hard part: (a) which blocks you request leaks the term prefix you scanned to whoever serves that core (the author's relay or the author online). Mitigation tier-1 (v1): request a small superset / pad with decoy term-prefix reads so the served pattern doesn't uniquely reveal the query; the served peer is by definition someone you already trust (a friend or a friend's relay), bounding the adversary. Mitigation tier-2 (later): batch-replicate a friend's whole small index (they are intentionally small — bounded to shared/endorsed pages) so you scan locally with zero per-query block-pattern leak. (b) The DHT IndexPointer lookup reveals WHICH friend's index you are resolving to the DHT — mitigated by resolving the whole frontier's pointers up front on app start, not per query, and by the contact-record fallback (no DHT hit at all for established contacts). PUBLISHING privacy: the user controls visibility per doc (shared / endorsed-only / private-never-published); raw browsing history is NEVER published by default — only explicit bookmarks and endorsements, the opposite of a surveillance index. RELAY query fast-path (the hyper-proxy relay race) is explicitly NOT used for queries: queries are P2P-only; the relay is used only to PIN/serve indexes, never to receive a plaintext query (avoiding YaCy's plaintext-Solr leak). This is also why we recommend flipping the hyper-proxy fetch to P2P-first generally. Hashed-terms/PIR are noted as a future research lane but explicitly out of v1 — the social bounding already makes the served adversary a trusted party.

**Incentives.** The honest answer: incentives are mostly ALIGNED BY DESIGN, not bolted on with a token, which is why this model sidesteps the free-rider death that killed volunteer P2P search. (1) You build and serve YOUR OWN index because it is YOUR search-over-what-you-browse — it works offline-first for you with zero peers, so you have a selfish reason to maintain it even if nobody queries you (the cold-start-via-own-data property doubles as the incentive). (2) You serve it to friends because reciprocity is social and visible — the result UI shows 'endorsed by Maya', so contributing your endorsements is a low-cost social good among people who already know each other; defection is socially observable in a way an anonymous DHT never is. (3) DURABILITY when you are offline is the one thing pure self-interest doesn't cover, and that is exactly what HiveRelay already solves: relays pin indexes (replicas:3) via the existing seed path. Relays are the durability FLOOR, not the crawl workers and not the ranking authority — avoiding both the ipfs-search single-operator collapse (multiple swappable relays via relay-directory, re-hostable by key) and pay-to-rank corruption (relays cannot influence ranking; they only store). (4) IF any explicit incentive is ever added it rewards SEEDING/DURABILITY only, as non-transferable expiring 'seed credit' redeemed as elevated swarm-grant standing (more bandwidth/rate-limit headroom on your own grants) — never a sellable asset, never a rank input, never an NFT-gated node. DURABILITY guarantee: index = seedable core; pinned to N relays + every querying friend who replicated it becomes a transient seeder; a popular doc's endorsing index is held by everyone who searched it (BitTorrent-style: popularity = redundancy).

**First slice.** SELF-SEARCH ONLY, ZERO NETWORK — ship a real engine over your own data in the smallest possible change. (1) Add a PersonalIndex Hyperbee exactly like Contacts: store.get({name:'pearbrowser-searchindex-v1'}), utf-8/json, in the user Corestore. (2) In UserData.addBookmark (and an addEndorsement call wired from a new 'endorse this page' browser action), after writing the bookmark, tokenize the page title + the HTML body bytes hyper-proxy already fetched, strip stopwords/lowercase, cap ~64 terms, and write the postings `t!<term>!<invScore>!<docId>` and forward doc `d!<docId>` rows (schemas above), signing each posting with identity.signForApp('search', payload, 'posting'). (3) Add a backend search(query) RPC that tokenizes the query, does the bounded range-scan-per-term + small intersection over the LOCAL PersonalIndex, scores with the pure Ranker (self = hop 0), and returns deduped results. (4) Wire a search box in the browser UI that calls it. NO swarm, NO DHT, NO relay, NO contacts in slice-1 — it is 100% local, private, deterministic, and immediately useful ('search everything I've bookmarked/endorsed, ranked'). This proves the index schema, the signing, the tokenizer, the pure ranker, and the result UI end-to-end. Slice-2 then adds exactly one network hop: announce your PersonalIndex on the swarm + pin it via the existing pinDriveBestEffort, replicate ONE contact's index (from Contacts), and merge their postings into the same scan — reusing the already-built ranker with hop-1 weighting. Slice-3 adds the DHT IndexPointer and multi-hop frontier. Each slice is independently shippable and each reuses the prior slice's engine unchanged.

**Killer risk.** Coverage starvation driven by graph sparsity: in a young network almost no one has enough contacts (or contacts who browse enough) for fan-out to return relevant results for anything outside what the user themselves already browsed — so the engine feels empty for general web queries exactly when first impressions are formed, and users fall back to the relay starter index, which quietly recentralizes the whole thing onto whatever the relay operator curated. The social-first model's coverage scales with graph density and breadth, which is precisely what a new P2P network lacks; if the bootstrap starter-index becomes the de-facto answer for most queries, Constellation degenerates into 'one relay's curated directory with extra steps' — the Almonit ceiling and the ipfs-search single-operator dependency reappearing through the back door. The honest test of the design is whether organic graph growth overtakes starter-index reliance before users conclude search 'doesn't work', and there is no guarantee it does.

# Pear-Native P2P Search: Shared Architecture Brief

## The shape of the problem

Content is addressed by opaque 32-byte keys. "Search" here is two distinct engines that must not be conflated:

1. **Discovery** — turning intent/keywords into the right `hyper://` keys. This is structurally a naming/directory problem (Zooko's triangle: a key is secure + decentralized but not human-meaningful). **It is shippable now** on primitives we already own.
2. **Full-text search** — classic ranked retrieval over P2P-published page bodies. This is where Sybil, ranking-without-authority, query privacy, and freshness all bite at once. **It is the hard, deferred half.**

The single most important strategic finding, convergent across all five dimensions: **do not build a global term-sharded DHT inverted index as the v1 ranking authority.** Every system that tried (YaCy) died on one of four things — multi-keyword AND intersection cost, gameable ranking, query-privacy leaks, and "who keeps the index alive." Every system that shipped usable search sidestepped the global distributed index (SearXNG federated meta-search owns no index; ipfs-search was a *central* crawler that died when its one operator ran out of funding; Almonit was a ~100-site curated client-side directory). The open research gap our stack can attack is making the *federation itself* P2P, signed, and durable.

## The convergent architecture (not yet a winner, but the shared frame)

Search as a **federation of many small, owner-published, signed, queryable indexes** — our schema-sheets rooms / Hyperbees — that the browser aggregates and **ranks client-side** over a social/relay trust graph. This is the Almonit "client-side search over a curated signed set" pattern, which is the only model that historically worked, mapped 1:1 onto infrastructure we already have (catalog aggregation, dedup, JMESPath, signed rows, relay pinning).

The building blocks each finding independently lands on:
- **Durability/who-stores-the-index** → HiveRelay. An index shard is just another seedable core; `hiveRelay.seed(keyHex,{replicas:3,discoveryKey})` (index.js:1582-1585) is the *exact* existing pin path. This is the answer YaCy (volunteer churn) and ipfs-search (single operator) never had — with **zero new relay code**.
- **Ranking without authority** → web-of-trust / social-graph rank (SybilGuard/SybilLimit/MeritRank/EigenTrust) over our **existing** signed identity + per-app trust tiers + relay reputation. Sybils are poorly connected to the honest graph. This replaces YaCy's trivially-gamed local page features.
- **Sybil/spam gate** → signed postings (per-app derived keys), per-writer-core isolation (drop a bad writer wholesale via read-time set-union), attestations (`addRowAttestation`), and a verification enum (unverified default / relay-listed / author-signed).
- **Query privacy** → local-first by construction: replicate the room, run JMESPath **on-device** (catalog-manager.js:118, sheets-catalog.js:191) — zero query leak. This already exists for the catalog.

## The 6-8 hardest cross-cutting tensions

1. **Index-placement vs privacy vs freshness.** Local-first query leaks nothing but can't hold the long tail / whole web. A relay HTTP fast-path gives cold-start speed and freshness but leaks the plaintext query to a semi-trusted relay (YaCy's plaintext-Solr mistake). DHT routing is fresh-ish but a non-durable 48h-TTL *hint*, never storage. There is no single placement that is private, fresh, and complete at once.

2. **Openness vs Sybil-resistance.** Almonit stayed tiny/curated and was spam-free; YaCy went open and got gamed. An open room anyone can write to is spammable; a curated room caps coverage and recreates a soft authority. Openness is only safe *after* the trust-graph rank is solid — and that rank has its own cold-start problem.

3. **Centralization-via-relay vs durability.** HiveRelay is the cleanest durability answer, but making relays the durability floor *and* primary crawl workers concentrates power (the ipfs-search single-operator failure mode). Mitigation must be **enforced, not optional**: relay-agnostic shard format (re-hostable by key), mandatory client-side re-verification of every relay claim (`verifyCapabilityDoc` "without trusting the room writer"), and a directory of multiple swappable relays. "The room is an index, not an authority."

4. **Multi-term AND scaling tax.** Fanning a keyword AND query across rooms and intersecting posting lists client-side *re-incurs* YaCy's scatter-gather + Zipf-hotspot cost. Bound fan-out to a trust-ranked subset, intersect only small pre-filtered candidate sets (per-shard Bloom prefilters), never the whole federation. **This single decision — single-room server-side vs trust-bounded client-side intersection vs facets-only — most determines whether we relive YaCy's scaling failure.**

5. **Freshness vs durability (stale-index poisoning).** Relay pinning fixes "index disappears" but a relay can serve a stale snapshot or a malicious writer can poison rows. Clients must re-verify per result (attestations, capability sigs, anchor proofs) *before* ranking. Freshness becomes a verifiable PoR-style challenge ("still hold shard X at length ≥ L crawled within T?"), with failed audits docking reputation.

6. **Incentive vacuum vs token corruption.** Free-riding is empirically fatal to volunteer P2P search (YaCy), yet token economies reintroduce pay-to-rank (Presearch keyword-staking) and NFT-gated recentralization. The resolution: durability floor lives on relays (not volunteers); any incentive must reward **durability/seeding, not rank**, and be non-transferable expiring "credit" redeemed as swarm-grant standing — never a sellable asset.

7. **Determinism vs latency.** Ranking incrementally as room results stream in (YaCy's arrival-time normalization) makes orderings flicker run-to-run. Ranking must be a **pure function** of the assembled candidate set + static trust graph, computed after a bounded collection window.

8. **Trust-graph coverage vs filter-bubble.** A web-of-trust rank has cold-start (a new user has no edges) and risks a popularity cartel / social filter bubble (the Freenet failure). Needs a bootstrap trust set (relay-listed + default-trusted publishers) that doesn't ossify into an authority.

## Non-negotiable constraints any Pear-native design must satisfy

- **No global term-sharded DHT inverted index as the v1 ranking authority.** DHT records expire (48h TTL, 20min refresh) — DHT holds tiny signed *pointers* only; postings live in relay-pinned Hyperbees/rooms.
- **The room/relay is an index, not an authority.** Every relay-served claim must be independently re-fetchable P2P and re-verifiable client-side (`verifyCapabilityDoc`, attestations, anchor proofs). A captured relay may degrade availability but must not forge or censor.
- **Local-first / on-device query is the default privacy posture.** Make the existing relay-vs-P2P fetch race **P2P-first** (hyper-proxy.js:678-694 currently leaks nearly every request to the default-on relay). Relay/HTTP query is opt-in, semi-trusted, rate-limited.
- **Every posting/row is signed** by a per-app derived key (`identity.signForApp`) with memberkey provenance; ranking and Sybil-filtering derive from signed trust + attestations, never publisher-controlled page features.
- **Ranking must be deterministic** — a pure function of candidate set + static trust graph, computed after a bounded window, not as-results-stream.
- **JMESPath/queries built only from whitelisted-field templates** with escaped literals and capped result size (existing `safeJmesPath` + `MAX_SHEETS_ROWS` chokepoint); never concatenate raw user text.
- **No pay-to-rank, no NFT-gated nodes.** Incentives (if any) reward durability/seeding only and stay optional.
- **Reuse, don't rebuild:** the index shard is the unit of storage/pinning/replication; one physical Hyperbee per shard (never one core per term — fatal for Corestore); reuse `createReadStream` range scans, `sub()`, `createDiffStream` for incremental freshness.

## The decisions still open (for the team, deliberately unresolved)

The unit of the "small signed index" (directory-of-keys for discovery vs per-publisher full-text); the concrete trust algorithm and its edge set/bootstrap; the cross-room AND strategy (#4); the query-privacy target for v1 (local-only vs relay fast-path; where, if ever, hashed-terms/PIR enter); the coverage/incentive model (curated-forever vs open + trust-rank); how much human-name→key aliasing to build; and the still-blocking shared question — **which relay z32 is the canonical baked-in bootstrap** (identical to the catalog's open question, TBD on the operator side).

# COMPLETENESS CRITIQUE — "Lighthouse" Synthesis Report

The report is unusually well-grounded; its three headline showstoppers (signForApp not drive/root-bound, corpus-can't-live-in-Autobase, proxy leaks every fetch) all re-verify cleanly in the repo, as does `MAX_SHEETS_ROWS` being unexported and `identity.verify` being absent. The gaps below are not in what it checked, but in what it asserted as "shippable now" without checking, numbers it never sanity-checked, and an entire attack class it omits.

## CRITICAL GAPS (block the "Phase 0 is pure assembly / Phases 0–1 shippable" claim)

**1. The single most-repeated feasibility claim — "discovery is ~90% assembly, Phase 0 is pure assembly" — is contradicted by the report's own verified facts and is never reconciled.** The report proves `list()` is an O(all-rows) in-memory `.toArray()`+JMESPath scan with *no honored limit*, then in §12/§14 says Phase 1 "reuse[s] `loadCatalogSheets`/`querySheetsCatalog` verbatim." Those are the *same* full-scan path. You cannot both (a) declare the schema-sheets read path architecturally unfit for scale and (b) reuse it verbatim for the discovery federation, even at "low-thousands of rows," without stating the row-count ceiling at which it becomes painful — which §13.8 admits is *unmeasured*. The "90% assembly" headline is unsupported: the report's own §3 net list ("genuinely new code: tokenizer, personal-index writer, inverted-index shard r/w, identity-binding + verify(), deterministic trust ranker, fan-out planner") is most of a search engine. **This is the report's central honesty failure: it adopts the reviews' showstoppers in the threat table but lets the optimistic "assembly" framing survive in the executive summary and roadmap.**

**2. The SwarmBridge cap (`maxChannelsPerApp:8`, `maxJoinsPerMinute:10`) is verified in the repo, flagged in TermShard's review as breaking fan-out in 1–2 queries — and the synthesized design never addresses it.** Lighthouse's query path (§6.5) fans out to "a trust-ranked subset" of frontier peers, each requiring a swarm join to replicate their personal index, plus DHT pointer resolution, plus (Phase 4) per-query-term shard joins. A hop-2 frontier of dozens of contacts blows an 8-channel / 10-join-per-minute budget instantly. The report inherited TermShard's showstopper-list but dropped *this specific quantified constraint* on the way to the hybrid. Either the search path bypasses the bridge (contradicting "through the existing chokepoint" discipline the report praises) or fan-out is capped at ~8 peers — which directly bounds the coverage the whole social-search thesis depends on. **Missing entirely; should be in the threat table and the fan-out planner spec.**

**3. "Pin the personal index via `hiveRelay.seed`" is repeated as the durability floor, but the report's own §10 caveat shows the relay AutoHeals only Hyperdrive-shaped cores — and then the roadmap (Phase 2) routes personal-index durability through option (c) "querier-as-transient-seeder + incidental replication," which is *not durability at all* when the user and all queriers are offline.** The report calls this "the durability floor" in the executive summary and §6.1, then quietly downgrades personal-index durability to best-effort incidental replication in §10/Phase 2. An offline user with no online querier is unreachable — i.e. *the cold-start personal index, the entire Constellation bootstrap win, has no durability story in the phase where it first ships.* The contradiction between "durability floor" (summary) and "design around offline gaps" (Phase 2) is never resolved; it is presented as a menu of three options with no commitment for the load-bearing tier.

## HIGH-SEVERITY GAPS

**4. Numbers that should be sanity-checked and aren't:**
- **"600ms collection window"** is asserted as the interactive budget but never reconciled with the cost it must cover: per-peer swarm connect + Hyperbee replication of B-tree path blocks + RowVerifier doing an ed25519 verify *plus a binding lookup* per candidate row. A cold swarm connection alone is often >600ms. No latency budget is decomposed. The one number that defines "interactive" is unsourced.
- **"≤64 terms/doc, top-N postings/term"** caps index *width* but the report never multiplies out personal-index *size*: 64 terms × N postings × (bytes/posting incl. a 64-byte sig + pubkey + json) × thousands of browsed docs. The §9 privacy mitigation ("batch-replicate the friend's *whole small* index") is load-bearing on the index staying small, and §11 storage caps are hand-waved as "mirroring MAX_HISTORY" — but `MAX_HISTORY` is a row count, not a byte budget for a signed inverted index. The "keeps full replication cheap" privacy claim rests on an unestimated number.
- **"256 shards (2 hex) → 4096 (3 hex) past ~10M docs"** — the 10M threshold is invented with no basis; a single hot Zipf prefix in a 256-fan-out holds a wildly disproportionate share, and the report's own §5 caveat admits "a single hot shard core can be large." The fan-out numbers are decorative.

**5. An entire attack modality is missing: the eclipse / replication-time withholding attack on the *verification* path, and DoS-by-forcing-verification.** The threat table covers omission (completeness/Bloom) and forgery (IdentityBinding), but not: (a) **a peer who serves a syntactically valid, signed, but stale or selectively-replicated B-tree** so the client's range scan silently misses postings that exist in the canonical core — Hyperbee sparse replication means the *serving* peer controls which blocks you see, and the report's "re-verify by independent Hyperbee get against the independently-replicated core" assumes you can get an *independent* replica, which for a single-author personal index you cannot (the author is the only writer); (b) **verification-cost DoS** — RowVerifier does a signature verify + binding resolution *per candidate row before ranking*; an attacker (or compromised contact) floods validly-signed junk specifically to exhaust the 600ms window with crypto, pushing real results out of the bounded set. The report rewards anonymous-spam impossibility but never costs the verification it mandates.

**6. The IdentityBinding primitive — the report's "one new mandatory primitive that fixes proposals 1/2/4" — has an unaddressed key-rotation / revocation hole and a trust-on-first-use gap.** The binding `{rootPubkey, searchSubPubkey, sig_by_root}` is published once. There is **no revocation path** if the search subkey's device is compromised (the subkey is deterministic from the root seed, so it *cannot* be rotated without rotating the root seed — a deeper problem the report never notices: `getAppKeypair('search')` is a pure function of the root seed, so a leaked search subkey is unrecoverable short of a new identity). And the *first* time you see a contact's binding you must trust it (their root pubkey is in your graph, but you fetch the binding from an untrusted index/DHT — a MITM substitutes a binding for an attacker subkey under a victim root pubkey unless the binding is fetched over a channel authenticated by the root key you already hold). The verification chain in §6.4 step 2 says "signed by a rootPubkey that is in the trust frontier" — but the sig is *by the root key*, which is correct, so this is salvageable; **the report just never states that the binding sig must be checked against the Contacts-held root pubkey, not a self-asserted one.** Rotation/revocation is simply absent.

## MEDIUM / VAGUER-THAN-THE-REST

**7. §8 ranking weights `w1…w5` are pure notation — no values, no normalization, no statement of how the five multiplicative factors compose without one term (e.g. a single huge `endorserBreadth`) dominating.** Multiplicative scoring with an unbounded `log(1+endorsers)` term and a BM25 term on different scales will produce degenerate orderings; the report is rigorous everywhere else and waves here. "Determinism ≠ global agreement" is honestly stated, but determinism of a *mis-scaled* function is worthless.

**8. The crawl/tokenizer is hand-waved on exactly the cases that break it.** "Tokenize the HTML body bytes hyper-proxy already fetched" ignores: non-HTML drives (the kind, by `resource.kind` enum, includes `dataset`/`app`/`feed`), JS-rendered content (Chromium renders it but the *proxy bytes* are pre-render), and non-Latin scripts (the §11 mitigation literally says "degrades for non-English" and then does nothing). For a P2P web of apps and drives, "index the bytes we fetched" indexes mostly unrendered shells.

**9. PoR freshness challenge ("return `t!` at key K from shard P at length ≥ L, crawled within T") is specified for shards but the report never says how a relay proves *freshness* (crawled-within-T) as opposed to *presence* — a relay can serve an old-but-valid posting forever.** Freshness PoR needs a signed recency commitment the report gestures at (`treeLength` vs manifest) but never closes; §13.2 lumps it into "open" while §10 lists it as a guardrail that makes incentives safe. It cannot be both a working guardrail and an open problem.

## Open research problems

- Cross-shard multi-keyword AND at interactive latency over Zipf-distributed posting lists on independent volunteer hosts — the determinative, unproven question (TermShard's declared killer). - Completeness / anti-omission under a captured relay or host: client re-verification proves authenticity, not completeness. A relay/host can silently withhold results. Per-epoch signed Bloom/MPHF docId commitments make omission sample-detectable, but a cheap robust completeness proof is open.
- Trust-graph cold-start without a soft authority: personalized rank collapses to the bootstrap/relay-listed set for a new user (the silent-recentralization failure HiveSearch and Constellation both name as their killer). - Query privacy beyond local-first: hashed terms over a low-entropy dictionary are reversible; the opt-in relay fast-path is honestly-not-private. - Drive-control attestation UX: requiring an in-drive /.well-known/lighthouse-attest to earn 'author-signed' tier is sound cryptographically but adds a publish-time step; the friction-vs-trust tradeoff and a smoother binding ceremony are open.

# Lighthouse v2 — Gap-closing addendum

*Delta to the synthesized report. Every primitive below was re-checked against the repo in this session (hypercore 11.28.1, hyperbee 2.27.3, autobase 7.27.3, hyperswarm 4.17.0, corestore 7.9.2) and against the new bench (`docs/research/bench-results-personal-index.md`). ## A. The two benchmark facts that reshape v2

1. **The local path is free; the budget is entirely networked.** Range-scan inverted index: single-term ~1.2 ms, 3-term AND ~4.48 ms at ~200k postings (bench rows 11–13). The "600 ms budget" is **not** a local-query budget — 100% of it is cold-swarm-connect + Hyperbee path-block replication + per-row verify. Every v2 latency and DoS argument is therefore about the *network + verify* half, which the bench explicitly left open.
2. **Per-posting signing breaks the two load-bearing claims of the social design.** ~425 B/posting, 25 postings/doc, 96 B fixed crypto (sig64+pub32) = 22.6% of each posting, duplicated 25× per doc. 8k docs ⇒ 199,683 postings ⇒ ~81 MB. This kills, as written: (a) §9's "batch-replicate a friend's whole small index and scan locally" privacy mechanism, and (b) §6.5-step-6's RowVerifier that ed25519-verifies *every* posting before ranking — 199,683 verifies ≈ 8 s of CPU, i.e. **any peer who ships you a fat index is a pre-ranking CPU-DoS**.

These two facts are the spine of the six deltas. Three of them (signing model, identity lifecycle, latency model) are now *shippable-grade* designs on verified primitives; two (withholding Layer-2, full digest fan-out at scale) remain *research-grade with honest residuals*; one (ranker) is *buildable now but with untuned constants*.

## B. Delta to §6.3 / §6.5 — **per-document signing + thin postings + digest tier** (closes the 81 MB / 8 s gap)

**Three coupled changes; the first two are a Phase-0 schema correction (no migration later), the third is a Phase-2/3 fan-out enabler.**

### B.1 Sign per-document, not per-posting (replaces §6.3-A and the §12 Phase-0 firstSlice "signed `signForApp(...,'posting')`")

The canonical per-doc payload — the *only* thing signed — is:
```
canonDoc = JSON-canonical (sorted keys, NO wall-clock) of {
  v:2, docId:sha256(driveKey ‖ '\0' ‖ path)[:16], driveKey, path,
  title<=200, terms:[{term,tf,field}] sorted by (term,field),
  contentHash, visibility, publishedAt }
signerPubkey = identity.getAppKeypair('search').publicKey
sig          = identity.signForApp('search', canonDoc, 'lighthouse-doc-v2')
```
Key-schema change to §6.3-A:
```
d!<docId>  -> { ...canonDoc fields, postingSetHash, sig, signerPubkey }   // ONE sig+pub PER DOC
t!<term>!<invScore>!<docId> -> { tf, fieldFlags }                         // THIN: drop sig/pub/driveKey/path/docId
        postingSetHash = sha256 over the sorted (term,field,tf) tuples
```
`signForApp` (identity.js:262, deterministic ed25519, 64 KB cap — canonDoc fits) is reused **unchanged**. The only crypto that moves is *out of* the 25 thin postings and *into* one `d!` record.

**Byte model (logical, with the 1.66× on-disk inflation factor back-derived from the bench's 425 on-disk / 256 logical):** thin `t!` posting drops ~196 B → ~3 B logical; per-posting on-disk ~425 → ~137 B; 8k docs **81 MB → ~26 MB (≈ −68%)**. *Honesty flag:* the 26 MB is a modeled −60–70%, **not** a re-measured number — a one-line bench variant (sign per-doc, thin `t!`) must confirm it **before Phase 2** (see roadmap Phase 2 gate).

### B.2 Per-DOC, LAZY verify (replaces §6.5-step-6 per-row RowVerifier)

For each candidate doc the range scan returns (bounded candidate window, ~200 docs/query):
1. `bee.get('d!'+docId)`.
2. recompute `postingSetHash` over the `t!` postings actually present; **require equality** — binds the thin postings to the signed set, so a peer cannot inject/alter a `t!` posting without breaking the hash.
3. `identity.verify(canonDoc, sig, signerPubkey)` — **the one genuinely new crypto method** (`sodium.crypto_sign_verify_detached`; confirmed absent: grep finds zero `crypto_sign_verify` in identity.js; the lone `.verify` at index-room-client.js:127 is an unrelated capability-doc check).
4. resolve `signerPubkey` through the IdentityBinding of a frontier root (§ D).

Verify is now **per-doc and lazy over the bounded candidate window: ~200 verifies ≈ 8 ms vs ~199,683 ≈ 8 s — a 1000× reduction** (= postings/candidate-docs, robust regardless of the absolute ~40 µs/op rate).

### B.3 Digest tier — replicated by default, full shard pulled only on a hit (the §9-privacy fix AND the fan-out enabler)

Each contact publishes one signed `meta!digest` record alongside the full index:
- a **docId Bloom** (p=0.1%, 14 bits/doc = 14 KB @ 8k docs) + a **top-2048-term doc-frequency head** (20 KB) = **~34 KB per contact (782× cheaper than the 26 MB full index)**.
- Protocol: replicate the 34 KB digest by default; on query term `t`, open a read-only replication of the peer's index core **only if** `digest.head` has `t` OR `digest.bloom.maybeContains(candidateDocIds)`; **otherwise skip the peer entirely** — zero block fetches, which closes the §9.2 block-request-pattern leak for non-matching peers (they never see a scan).

### B.4 Byte-budget LRU eviction (generalizes the verified user-data.js:143-149 idiom)

On each posting write, if `indexBytes > CAP` (default 32 MB), scan `t!` ascending by `invScore` (lowest `localScore` first, `reverse:true` on the inverted key), `del()` postings + orphaned `d!` records until under CAP. This is the **exact** `user-data.js:143-149` overflow-collect-then-`del` template, generalized from row-count→byte-count and oldest-first→lowest-score-first.

**Residual (honest):** (1) `postingSetHash` binds postings to a doc but a peer can still **omit** a `t!` term without detection unless the querier derefs `d!` and notices the term is in signed `terms[]` but absent from `t!` — completeness is checkable only on docs you deref (the § E omission threat, now at posting granularity). (2) Bloom p=0.1% ⇒ ~0.1% wasted shard pulls. (3) LRU trades recall for bytes: a friend's long-tail/older docs silently drop from fan-out. (4) Per-doc signing means a tf-inflation attack inside an honestly-signed doc is one sig to forge, not 25 — mitigated only because the ranker already gives page-controlled tf zero independent weight (§8.6) and trust-rank caps unknown publishers. (5) `identity.verify` is new crypto surface — **must** domain-separate exactly as `signForApp`'s tag (`pear.app.search:lighthouse-doc-v2:`) or cross-context replay reopens.

## C. Delta to §6.5-step-2/4 / §11 — **search joins the shared swarm directly under its own SearchFanoutBudget, never SwarmBridge** (closes the 8-channel/10-join cap blocker)

### C.1 The load-bearing repo fact (verified this session)

The SwarmBridge caps `maxChannelsPerApp:8` / `maxJoinsPerMinute:10` (swarm-bridge.js:33-34) are **NOT a global swarm limit** — they are a **per-page-`driveKeyHex` throttle on untrusted webview joins** routed through the bridge. `join()` validates a 64-hex `driveKeyHex` from the page's API token and `_enforceJoinRate`/`_enforceChannelCount` key on it (swarm-bridge.js:94-99). **Every first-party backend subsystem already bypasses the bridge** and calls `this.swarm.join()` directly on the one shared `Hyperswarm` (index.js:1536), which replicates via `store.replicate(conn)` (index.js:1549): verified at catalog-manager.js:37,191,285,564,574; app-manager.js:36,96; index-room-client.js:79; sheets-catalog.js:139; pear-bridge.js:76,109 — **~10+ direct joins at boot.**

**Therefore:** Lighthouse search is a first-party module, identical in trust posture to CatalogManager, and must join the shared swarm directly under its **own** `SearchFanoutBudget`. This is **not** "raising the cap" — it is recognizing the thing the bridge exists to contain (untrusted page code minting topics) is not search. The bridge stays byte-for-byte unchanged.

### C.2 SearchFanoutBudget (a small new class, search-scoped)

```
MAX_LIVE_REPLICATION_SESSIONS = 24   // LRU pool of contact-index cores held open across queries
MAX_NEW_CONNECTS_PER_QUERY    = 4    // hard cap, provable per query
MAX_NEW_JOINS_PER_MINUTE      = 30   // search-subsystem-scoped, INDEPENDENT of the page bridge's 10
DIGEST_FANOUT_PER_QUERY       = 64   // digests are 34 KB → cheap to keep warm
```
Four mechanisms, in priority order:
- **(A) Connection reuse / long-lived sessions.** Resolve the whole frontier's index keys **once at app start** (batch DHT `mutableGet` + Contacts fallback, §6.5-step-4). Hold an LRU pool of ≤24 open read-only replication sessions. Hyperswarm `join()` is idempotent + connection-pooled (one swarm, refcounted topics — the same `swarm.on('connection')→store.replicate` pool the managers share), so a **warm contact costs 0 new connects, 0 new joins** at query time. hop-0 answers locally (zero network). Steady state: a query touches 15–40 contacts but opens **0** new connections.
- **(B) Digest-first fan-out (the coverage unlock).** Probe up to 64 warm 34 KB digests locally (Bloom membership, ~free like the 1 ms scans); open full replication only to the ≤4 peers whose digest signals a hit. **You "query" 64 peers, open real channels to ~3** — coverage scales past 8 without touching the connect budget.
- **(C) Bounded top-N frontier + LRU.** Frontier trust-ranked (`socialProximity 1/(hop+1) × tierWeight` from swarm-grant A/B/C). Digest pool admits top-N by trust; live full-replication pool is LRU over 24 slots; **mid-query cores are pinned** (never evict a core with an in-flight scan — the §7 storage-discipline rule).
- **(D) Per-query connect algorithm (provably ≤4 new connects):**
```
candidates = digestProbe(query.terms, warmDigests[top 64 by trust])   // local Bloom, ~free
hits = candidates.filter(bloomHit).sortByTrust()
opened = 0
for peer in hits:
  if peer.coreWarm: scanLocal(peer); continue           // 0 connects (reuse)
  if opened >= 4: break                                  // hard cap
  if !budget.canJoin(): break                            // 30/min search-scoped
  openReplication(peer); opened++; budget.recordJoin()
rankAfter(250ms first-paint OR all-opened-replicated)    // § F two-phase window
```

**Residual (honest):** (1) digest replication itself reveals interest in those contacts (same residual as §9's DHT-pointer leak, bounded to trusted contacts). (2) A **cold first session** (empty LRU, no warm digests) bursts toward the 30/min budget; query #1 coverage is degraded until digests warm — hop-0 still answers. (3) The 24-session pool has real memory/bandwidth cost; needs the §7 byte-cap. (4) This solves the **connection** budget, **not** trust-graph sparsity for a <5-contact user (the §11 cold-start row, unchanged). (5) **24 / 4 / 30 are reasoned from the per-app=8 baseline, not measured** under real swarm tail latency — needs the fan-out micro-bench the bench-results doc left open.

## D. Delta to §6.4 / §11 (Posting forgery row) — **versioned IdentityBinding lifecycle: rotatable key + revocation + first-use auth** (turns the §6.4 one-shot record into a real binding)

§6.4 as written has three load-bearing holes: **no first-use auth** (if the verifier trusts the rootPubkey *inside* the binding, an attacker self-signs a valid binding — circular); **not rotatable** (`getAppKeypair('search')` is `SHA-256(rootSeed ‖ "pear-app-v1:" ‖ driveKey)`, deterministic — the search subkey is byte-identical forever and across devices, so a leak is unrecoverable without `rotate()` which orphans all data, identity.js:325); **no revocation/version** (a leaked-but-valid `signForApp('search',…)` ranks forever). Fix with a versioned lifecycle over three records, all on verified primitives + the one new `identity.verify()`.

### D.1 Rotatable key (fix "not rotatable")

**Stop deriving the search key from the seed.** On first run, `sodium.crypto_sign_keypair()` → a **random** ed25519 keypair persisted in a dedicated Corestore core `store.get({name:'lighthouse-search-identity-v1'})`, NOT the BIP-39 seed. Root seed still derives the root keypair (identity.js:208, never leaves the worklet). Rotation = generate a fresh random keypair, write `sk!<v+1>`, bump the binding version — a new keypair exists **without touching root identity**.

### D.2 The three records (canonical JSON, sorted keys, detached ed25519 hex)
```
IdentityBinding_v2 = { v:2, rootPubkey:hex32, searchPubkey:hex32, version:int>=1,
  createdAt:int, prevSearchPubkey:hex32|null, domain:'lighthouse-binding-v2', sig_by_root:hex64 }
SearchKeyRevocation_v2 = { v:2, rootPubkey:hex32, revokedSearchPubkey:hex32, revokedVersion:int,
  reason:'leak'|'rotation'|'retire', revokedAt:int, domain:'lighthouse-revocation-v2', sig_by_root:hex64 }
```
`sig_by_root = identity.sign(canonical).signature` — the **ROOT** key (identity.js:292), **NOT** `signForApp`. Justified because Contacts is keyed on root pubkey (contacts.js:12), so only a root signature can be checked against the frontier. `version` is monotonic per rootPubkey; `prevSearchPubkey` chains rotations. A rotation publishes **both** binding `v+1` AND a revocation of the old key, so a verifier learns "old key dead" even if it never sees the new binding.

### D.3 Verify algorithm (replaces §6.5-step-6 binding check), per signer, amortized across that signer's docs:
1. **Frontier gate (fixes first-use auth):** find binding(s) whose `searchPubkey === posting.signerPubkey`; read claimed `rootPubkey`; require `rootPubkey ∈ TrustFrontier` where frontier roots come **ONLY** from `Contacts.list()`/swarm-grants (contacts.js root-keyed), **NEVER** from the binding itself. No frontier root claims it → drop.
2. **Binding sig:** `identity.verify(canonical(binding), sig_by_root, rootPubkey)` against the **frontier-held** root pubkey.
3. **Revocation gate:** if a revocation for `rootPubkey` has `revokedSearchPubkey === signerPubkey` AND `revokedAt <= posting.publishedAt` → drop (signed after revocation = forged). Postings with `publishedAt < revokedAt` are tier-capped, not dropped (a leak doesn't erase honest history).
4. **Supersession:** keep only `max(version)`; non-current searchPubkey outside rotation grace → tier-cap to `unverified`.
5. **Posting sig:** verify against `signerPubkey`. Only now does the doc enter the candidate set at full tier.

### D.4 Propagation (belt-and-suspenders, both on verified patterns)
- **DHT:** `dht.mutablePut(rootKeypair, canonical(record), {seq:version})`; `mutableGet(rootPubkey)` auto-verifies the sig against the root pubkey (relay-record.js:6-9: a malicious DHT node can only serve **stale**, never forge). `seq=version` = free monotonic supersession; 48 h TTL = the trust-on-stale window. Batch-resolved at app start (§6.5-step-4), so revocation costs no per-query DHT hit.
- **Descriptor room:** add `binding`/`revocation` schemas (bounded, low-thousands rows, comfortably under the 10k/22 ms `list()` cliff — bench §C); verified via the index-room-client.js:120-141 verify-and-drop loop: `verify(doc) = identity.verify(canonical, doc.sig_by_root, doc.rootPubkey) && doc.rootPubkey ∈ frontier`. Relay-pinned so it survives the owner being offline when their key leaks.
- **Trust-on-stale window:** a binding not re-confirmed within `maxAge` (default 48 h = the DHT mutable TTL) drops full-tier → `relay-listed` (rank-capped, not dropped — recall over correctness, §7). On each query, opportunistically `mutableGet` frontier roots whose cached binding is older than `maxAge/2`.

**New `identity` API:** `verify(payload, sigHex, pubKeyHex)` and `rotateSearchKey(reason)`. **Residual (honest):** (1) leak-before-revocation-propagates window (≤48 h) is irreducible for any async revocation. (2) **Root-key compromise is still terminal** — `rotate()` orphans data and breaks every Contacts edge; this design rotates the *search* key cheaply, it does NOT solve root recovery (out of scope, stated plainly). (3) A fully eclipsed verifier (offline + censoring relay + no DHT) can't learn a revocation — falls back to maxAge tier-cap, not a hard drop. (4) The random search secretKey is a new at-rest secret in Corestore beyond the seed — must inherit the seed's storage protections; device-disk compromise leaks it (but revocation then recovers, which the old seed-derived key could not).

## E. Delta to §10 / §11 (Completeness row) / §13.2 — **completeness anchor: Layer-1 provable prefix + Layer-2 sample-detectable withholding** (makes the §10 "cannot censor undetectably" claim TRUE)

§10 currently overclaims: "a captured relay cannot forge or censor undetectably" is **false** for censorship-by-omission — per-row sig checks catch forged rows but are blind to rows simply not shown. Fix on hypercore 11.28.1's already-installed signed-tree machinery (verified: `core.signedLength` index.js:602, `core.signable(length,fork)` 1110 = `caps.treeSignable`, `core.treeHash` 1118, `core.verifyFullyRemote` 1149, and `lib/fully-remote-proof.js` whose comment says it is "for fully remote proofs… where no other context exists" — exactly a cold-verifiable completeness proof).

### E.1 Layer 1 — provable prefix integrity (cryptographic, not sampled)

Because a Hyperbee is one Hypercore and `bee.version === Math.max(1, core.length)` (verified), "the author's index up to length L" is a precise author-SIGNED object. The author publishes a signed **CompletenessAnchor**:
```
{ indexKey, epoch, fork, signedLength:L, treeHash(=core.treeHash(L)),
  treeSig(=core's author sig over treeSignable), docCount,
  docSetCommit:{kind:'bloom', m, k, seedHex, bytes}    // BLAKE2b Bloom over docIds @ L, ~14 KB @8k/1% FP
  probeSalt, sig_by_searchSubkey }
```
A client verifies the anchor with `core.verifyFullyRemote` against the IdentityBinding-bound subkey with **ZERO trust in the serving peer**. **Provable:** "I am looking at the author's committed tree at length L, not a truncated/forked tree" — a serving peer cannot show a shorter-but-claimed-complete tree without an invalid signature, and cannot fork without changing `fork`.

### E.2 Layer 2 — sample-detectable withholding (statistical, against intra-tree omission)

Client picks R=20 random docIds from `docSetCommit` (derived `HKDF(probeSalt ‖ queryNonce)` so the server can't pre-game), and over a new Protomux channel `search/complete-v1` demands every `t!…!<d>` row at `bee.checkout(L)` **plus** a `core.proof({upgrade:{start:0,length:L}})` path-block proof, verified by `core.verifyFullyRemote` against the signed `treeHash@L`. Withholding a posting for `d` means the peer must either return it or produce a non-forgeable **absence proof** at the signed tree. **A peer withholding fraction f is caught with probability 1−(1−f)^R** (R=20 catches 10% omission ~88%).

**Provable vs sampled, stated precisely:** *provable* = tree integrity, no-truncation, no-fork, no-substitution, and per-probed-key presence/absence at signed L. *Sample-detectable only* = bulk silent omission of un-probed keys, and cross-replica equivocation on the SET (caught only by cross-checking anchors across the ≥3 replicas §11 already mandates — divergent signed anchors at the same (epoch,L) are themselves signed proof of author equivocation).

**Residual (honest, and a true limit of single-writer indexes):** (1) **Targeted micro-omission below the probe rate** — omitting one rival's docId evades random probes with prob (1−targetFrac)^R; fundamentally sample-detectable, never provable, for un-probed keys. (2) **Author-level omission** — the anchor proves consistency with what the author committed at L; if the single author never indexed a doc, no proof exists vs ground truth. Completeness is defined *only relative to the author's committed set*. This cannot be closed without a second independent writer/auditor — the honest boundary, and why §13.2 stays a research item for the global case even though Layer-1 is now shippable. (3) Equivocation only caught if the client actually fetches ≥2 replicas' anchors. (4) Layer-2 probing reveals interest in those docIds to the serving peer (bounded — personal-index tier is a trusted contact; shard tier is a relay). (5) Bloom 1% FP lets a peer "prove presence" of an uncommitted docId rarely — MPHF removes it at higher build cost (a tunable). **Prerequisite:** fix the `MAX_SHEETS_ROWS` export bug (imported index-room-client.js:17, never exported sheets-catalog.js:219) so the anchor-row read path is bounded.

## F. Delta to §6.5-step-7 / §8 / §11 — **three-tier latency model + two-phase window + verify cap** (justifies "600 ms" and bounds the verify-DoS)

§6.5-step-7's "e.g. 600 ms or N peers" is a bare constant; the threat model never caps verify. The bench proves the local path is ~5 ms, so the whole budget is network + verify, and (per § B.2) an un-capped per-doc verify loop is a DoS. Replace with:

### F.1 Hop classification (only hop-0 is interactive)
- **hop-0:** local range-scan ~5 ms, own postings (trust your own subkey, §12 Phase 0) → render at ~5–15 ms.
- **hop-1 warm** (pointer pre-resolved at app start, core already replicating): digest scan ~1 ms + verify K matched docs → budget ~50–150 ms, renders in window.
- **hop-1 cold** (must connect): cold-swarm-connect dominates (200–2000 ms+ tail; the repo already allots **8 s** for relay race and **15 s** for `drive.get({wait:true})`, hyper-proxy.js — verified; `_discovery.flushed` is best-effort with no sub-second guarantee) → **demote to background stream**.
- **hop-N:** cold connect + full shard pull + verify → seconds → **strictly background**, never blocks first paint. This is **forced** by the 8-channel/10-join cap on cold joins — you cannot cold-connect a wide frontier inside 250 ms.

### F.2 Two-phase collection window (the "600 ms" is now justified)
- **Phase-A "first paint":** hard **250 ms** wall-clock OR hop-0 + all warm hop-1 answered (whichever first). hop-0 renders at ~10 ms; warm hop-1 splices in as verified rows arrive. **The interactive budget is 250 ms**, of which hop-0 is ~10 ms.
- **Phase-B "enrichment":** soft **600 ms** (default; raised to ~2 s if the user keeps the result open) background window for cold hop-1 + hop-N; results stream and re-sort. **"600 ms" = the Phase-B soft cap, NOT the interactive budget.**
- A **deterministic re-rank runs exactly once at each window close** (250 ms, then 600 ms) over the accumulated *verified* set using the pure §8 ranker with the query-time freshness constant — so ordering never flickers per-arrival (kills YaCy's arrival-time flicker) and is reproducible.

### F.3 Verify cap (the DoS bound)
`MAX_VERIFIES_PER_WINDOW = 256` ed25519 verifies/query (~one digest/peer + top candidates) ≈ **13 ms at ~50 µs/verify**, safely inside the 250 ms cap. Verifies are spent **digest-first** (1 per peer) then per-doc on **already-matched** candidates only, in trust order (hop-0 → warm hop-1 → by socialProximity); the loop **yields to the event loop every ~32 verifies** so it never blocks render. A junk peer fails its single digest verify (or isn't in the frontier) and is dropped after **exactly 1 verify** — it cannot force more than 1 verify until it produces a Bloom hit, then is capped by the per-window budget. Budget exhausted → remaining peers dropped with a "partial results" provenance chip.

**Residual (honest):** (1) cold-connect tail is unbounded by Hyperswarm holepunch variance — v2 **hides** it (background stream) rather than fixing it; a hard-NAT peer may never answer (accepted recall loss, §7). (2) The 256 cap systematically drops the least-trusted tail for a huge frontier (signalled by the chip). (3) The one number **not yet measured** is warm-hop-1 path-block replication latency — the bench covered local query + index bytes, not cross-peer Hyperbee path-block fetch. **That single measurement (replicate one contact's digest + range-scan their shard, cold vs warm) is the gate that locks the 250 ms cap and is the next bench to run before Phase 2.**

## G. Delta to §8 — **concrete capped-additive ranker** (turns the notation into a buildable, degeneracy-free spec)

§8's `score = w1·socialProximity × w2·endorserBreadth × … × w5·freshness` is pure notation with three defects: (1) no weights/normalization/units across incommensurate scales; (2) **multiplicative with an unbounded `log(1+endorsers)`** → one 50-endorser doc swamps everything (the §11 popularity-cartel failure) while any single zero feature annihilates the score; (3) `freshness` reads wall-clock, violating the autobee no-wall-clock-in-fold discipline (autobee-catalog-apply.cjs). Replace with a **capped, normalized, additive-in-log-space** pure function `rank(C, G, now0)`, `now0` a single query-time integer passed in (never read in the fold).

Five features each normalized to [0,1], summed in log-space with an ε-floor, blended with deterministic exploration:
```
W=[1.0,1.6,0.8,0.5,0.6]; EPS=0.02; GAMMA=0.15; LAMBDA=0.3; K1=4.0; E_CAP=8; HALFLIFE=14d
f1 text     = BM25/(BM25+K1)                                   // saturating squash, →[0,1)
f2 trust    = clamp01(personalized-PageRank-lite over G)       // 12-iter power-iteration, α=0.15, seed {me}; fallback 1/(hop+1)
f3 endorsers= log(1+min(n,E_CAP))/log(1+E_CAP)  E_CAP=8        // HARD CAP — the unbounded-term fix
f4 recency  = 2^(-max(0,now0-publishedAt)/HALFLIFE)            // the ONLY use of now0, a constant arg
f5 tier     = {A:1.0,B:.66,C:.33,unverified:.15}[d.tier]
base(d)  = Σ Wi·log(EPS+fi)                                     // ε-floor: a weak feature damps but never annihilates
score(d) = (1-GAMMA)·base(d) + GAMMA·explore(d)
```
**Exploration without breaking determinism:** `explore(d)` is a stable per-doc dither `u=(fnv1a(contentHash) mod 1000)/1000` lifted into the observed `[base_min, base_max]` band, plus a deterministic greedy **MMR** diversity demote `−LAMBDA·maxTermSim(d, picked)`. Total tie-break `(−score, contentHash, signerPubkey)` (reuses the autobee-catalog-apply.cjs `cmp` idiom). Same inputs → byte-identical ranking forever. The per-query power-iteration (≤10k root edges) + O(|C|²) MMR (|C| bounded by fan-out to tens) sit comfortably inside the budget the bench shows is entirely networked.

`f2`'s `signerRoot` is resolved **via IdentityBinding** (§ D), since `signForApp` does not yield the root pubkey Contacts is keyed on. `f1` uses only signed posting fields (`tf`/`titleHit`) — **never** page-asserted (§8.6). `f4` cap E_CAP=8 is the direct numeric fix to §8's unbounded term.

**Residual (honest):** (1) PPR over a sparse young graph collapses toward {me} — the cold-start threat is *damped* (f2 is one of five bounded features, not a multiplier) but **not eliminated**; a 0-contact user ranks on f1·f4·f5 over own data (acceptable for a personalized engine, not a global relevance guarantee). (2) **The constants W/γ/E/H/λ are principled defaults, NOT tuned** against a labeled relevance corpus (which does not yet exist) — present them as the operating point, not optimal; each is a single swappable client-baked number, so the determinism contract holds for any values. (3) `f3` endorser integrity is **hard-gated on IdentityBinding (Phase 2)** — until then `endorserCount` MUST be forced to 0 / `f3` to the ε-floor for any non-hop-0 candidate, or a Sybil forges breadth.
