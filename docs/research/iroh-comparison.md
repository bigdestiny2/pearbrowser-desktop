## 1. Summary

iroh (n0-computer) and the Holepunch / Pear stack are two of the very few production P2P frameworks that solve the same hard problem — **dial a public key, not an IP; holepunch through NATs; fall back to a relay; sync verified data** — and they reach strikingly convergent conclusions from opposite directions. Both are **Ed25519-keyed**, both treat **discovery as "resolve a key → addrs"**, both run an **always-on relay backbone** for rendezvous + fallback, and both ship **Merkle-verified streaming** data layers. They diverge most where it matters for *us*: iroh stands on **QUIC** (multipath, one connection, holepunch-inside-the-transport) while Pear stands on **Hyperswarm + a Noise/UDX transport**; iroh's data model is **content-addressed immutable blobs + a separate mutable index** (blobs/docs), while Pear's is a **key-addressed, ordered, signed append-log** (Hypercore/Autobase). Neither has any payments story.

The headline takeaway: **iroh is more mature *below* the data layer (transport, discovery, relay), and Holepunch is more mature *at and above* it (append-logs, multi-writer linearization via Autobase, a shipping app runtime).** Our stack has *already* made one iroh-shaped move — the `feat/phase5-relay-directory` branch resolves relays by **pubkey over the DHT** with a **self-certifying signed record** (`backend/relay-record.js` `mutableGet` → verified Ed25519), which is structurally the same idea as iroh's **Pkarr "dial-by-key, discover-the-path"** model (the briefing even calls our BOOTSTRAP_RELAYS an "iroh-inspired move"). This study maps what *else* is borrowable, per research track, onto primitives we already have.

The borrowable lessons cluster into four moves: (1) adopt iroh's **signed-DNS/Pkarr-over-mainline-DHT discovery shape** as the *transport* for our naming pointer records and relay directory — it is our `relay-record.js` pattern generalized and battle-tested; (2) reframe HiveRelay against iroh's **DERP relay metadata model** — be explicit that "9/10 go direct, relay sees who-talks-to-whom + bytes only pre-holepunch, never content," which is a *target* our default-on plaintext relay does not yet meet; (3) treat **iroh-gossip (HyParView+PlumTree)** as the reference epidemic overlay our Nostr-bridge gossip path should imitate, while keeping our Autobee log for durability/replay (iroh-gossip deliberately has neither); (4) **don't borrow iroh-docs' under-specified conflict semantics** — our Autobase/Autobee deterministic-reducer-with-author-written-apply-functions is the *more* principled multi-writer model, and the comparison sharpens *why*.

## 3. Concrete lessons per research track

### 3.1 Naming — `naming.md` (human names → keys)

iroh's discovery layer is the closest production analogue to what `naming.md` proposes, and it validates the design's core bet.

- **Pkarr is `relay-record.js` generalized — and it works at scale.** iroh's endpoints publish **signed, key-addressed, self-certifying** records that a client resolves by knowing only the key. That is *exactly* our `resolveRelayRecord(dht, pubkey)` → verified `mutableGet`. `naming.md`'s §7.2 name-record ("reuse `relay-record.js` verbatim in shape") is therefore on proven ground: **a signed pointer record on a DHT, verified against the key, is the right substrate.** iroh ships two transports for it — **DNS (cacheable, server-assisted, default)** and **mainline DHT BEP44 (pure-P2P, opt-in)**. We should plan for *both* shapes too: a fast cacheable path and a censorship-resistant DHT path.
- **The hard honesty iroh states out loud: "discovery alone cannot bootstrap initial connection."** You must already *know* the key. This is precisely `naming.md`'s petname-first thesis — the **key is the source of truth**; a human name is only ever a *trust-relative binding* (your petnames, curators you follow, a decaying baked-in set), **never a global first-claim registrar**. iroh squares Zooko's triangle the same way we do: secure+global key, plus a *separately-trusted* human-readable layer. Our design is aligned; iroh is the precedent to cite.
- **Borrow the wire shape.** iroh's TXT record carries `relay=<url>` + optional `addr=<addr>` keyed by a **z-base-32** encoding of the EndpointId. For `naming.md`'s pointer records, adopt the same **"z32 key → {endpoint hints}"** envelope and the **signed-record-so-publisher-is-verifiable** invariant. The verify-and-drop ingest (`relay-directory.js:34-39`, `index-room-client.js:126-127`) is our equivalent of "records are signed → reject forgeries."
- **One key, many roles** (iroh §2): the *same* Ed25519 key is the dial target, the discovery-record signer, and the relay-auth credential. Our per-app subkey derivation (`identity.js` `getAppKeypair`) is the more privacy-preserving generalization — keep it, and extend the same domain-separated derivation to **name-ownership keys** (`naming.md` §7.4 already proposes a new domain separator).

### 3.2 Privacy-routing — `privacy-routing.md` (anonymity over Hyperswarm)

iroh's relay model is the clearest target for what "metadata-minimizing by default" should *mean*, and it exposes the gap in our current default-on relay.

- **iroh's relay metadata profile is the bar to hit.** Because every iroh connection is **e2e-encrypted** and **~9/10 go direct**, an iroh relay can see only **"EndpointId X talks to Y + byte counts, and only until a direct path forms — never content."** Our HiveRelay today is the opposite: it **terminates plaintext HTTP**, sees **`{drive key, path, time, source IP}` on ~every fetch** (`privacy-routing.md` §3.1, gap 2), and **pins your data**. The borrowable lesson is the *target shape*: **forwarding relays should forward ciphertext, see only coarse who-talks-to-whom metadata, and drop out once a direct path exists.** This directly informs Layer 0 ("prefer direct; touch the relay only when direct fails") and Layer 1 (single-hop relay-through carrying ciphertext, not terminating plaintext).
- **Holepunch *inside* the encrypted/congestion-controlled channel.** iroh is moving NAT-traversal packets **into QUIC** so they inherit encryption, loss recovery, congestion control, and DDoS protection. The principle transfers regardless of transport: when `privacy-routing.md` Layer 1/2 adds relay-through or onion hops, **carry them under the existing Noise channel**, not as side-band UDP that an observer can fingerprint or that congestion can starve.
- **Multipath as path-selection policy.** iroh's `RemoteStateActor` does **bias/quality-based path selection across relay + direct + multiple interfaces under one connection.** Reframe our `_doHybridFetch` "fire both, take first" race as **"prefer the lowest-leakage path, fall back deliberately"** — the relay leg should be a *fallback*, not a default-concurrent fire. This is a config/policy change, not a transport rewrite.
- **Relay access control.** `iroh-relay`'s **EndpointId allow/deny lists + shared tokens + HTTP-callout auth** (and the rc.1 `AccessControl` trait with `on_connect`/`on_disconnect` hooks) is the model for a **routing-node directory's** admission control — map onto our `swarm-grants.js` consent tiers and the verify-and-drop directory (`relay-directory.js`). **QAD (QUIC Address Discovery)** replacing classic STUN is the "discover your own reflexive address without a separate STUN service" idea worth noting.
- **Honest limit iroh shares:** even iroh's relay sees coarse metadata in the pre-holepunch window, and a relay that *also pins your data* (as HiveRelay does) is a near-complete metadata view — `privacy-routing.md` §3.1's gap 9 ("HiveRelay backbone is a black box") stands. iroh does **not** give us onion/mixnet anonymity (it has no cover traffic, no multi-hop unlinkability); that remains our Layer 2 net-new work.

### 3.3 Nostr-bridge — `nostr-bridge.md` (gossip + sync)

iroh-gossip and iroh-docs are the two iroh pieces that map onto the bridge's transport and store, and the comparison sharpens both our design choices.

- **iroh-gossip is the reference for a live epidemic overlay.** `nostr-bridge.md` §6 candidate C ("Autobase multi-writer relay-room, gossip-style") and §7.3's transport layer want exactly what iroh-gossip provides: **fan a signed event to everyone subscribed to a topic.** If/when we build a live overlay rather than relying on `wss://` relays or pull-from-store, **imitate HyParView (partial-view membership) + PlumTree (epidemic broadcast trees)** rather than naive flood-to-all over a Hyperswarm topic — they bound redundancy and membership cost.
- **But keep the two things iroh-gossip deliberately omits: durability and payload signing.** iroh-gossip is a *live broadcast only* — no replay, no history (those come from blobs/docs). Our design already does the right thing by storing events in the **Autobee event log** (`nostr-bridge.md` §7.2, `nostr-events-{ops,apply,store}.cjs`) for durability/replay and **Schnorr-signing every NIP-01 event** (iroh-gossip has *no* documented per-message signing — authenticity there rests only on the QUIC channel). **Do not copy iroh-gossip's unsigned-payload model** — Nostr's whole trust story is per-event signatures, which we verify on ingest (`validateEvent` recomputes `id` + Schnorr-verifies, mirroring the verify-and-drop loop).
- **iroh-docs ↔ Autobee: borrow the sync, reject the conflict model.** iroh-docs is a **multi-writer signed KV** (Namespace write-cap + per-Author signatures, every entry signed with both) synced by **range-based set reconciliation (Meyer's algorithm)** — and that reconciliation is a *genuinely better diff primitive* than full-log replication for large event sets. But its **conflict semantics are under-specified** (author + timestamp discriminators, no explicit CRDT/LWW statement). Our event reducer (`nostr-events-apply.cjs`) is the stronger answer: **order by writer+seq / monotonic epoch, never `created_at`; `created_at` is display-only**; replaceable kinds resolve by deterministic order so **a writer with a skewed clock cannot win** — precisely the property a timestamp-LWW design lacks. The dual-curve binding (`nostr-bridge.md` §7.1) — Ed25519 root *and* secp256k1 nsec both sign the same canonical bytes, accept only if both verify — has no iroh analogue and is *ahead* of iroh-docs' single-namespace-key model.
- **Status caveat to honor:** iroh-docs is the **least-committed** iroh protocol (v0.100.0, no 1.0 target, Willow is the experimental successor). If we lean on iroh-docs concepts, lean on the *reconciliation algorithm* (mature idea) not the *crate* (unstable).

### 3.4 Payments — `payments.md`

**iroh has no payments layer of any kind.** There is no wallet, no settlement, no invoice, no value-transfer primitive anywhere in iroh core or its companion crates. The comparison is therefore a **negative result**, and a useful one:

- **There is no iroh prior-art to borrow for `payments.md`.** Our payments design (non-custodial settlement adapters behind a POS processor seam, **signed-receipt Autobee op-log**, self-certifying merchant binding — `payments.md` §7.1–7.3) is net-new relative to iroh. The closest *transferable* idea is structural, not financial: payments rides the **same signed-record + deterministic-reducer spine** as everything else (a receipt is just another signed, monotonic-epoch-ordered op in an Autobee log), and the **merchant binding** is the same self-certifying `relay-record.js`/pkarr pattern applied to a merchant key.
- **iroh's discovery/relay layer is still relevant *as plumbing* for payments** even though iroh itself does no payments: dialing a **merchant by key** (pkarr-style), holepunching to it, and falling back to a relay are exactly the transport needs of a P2P point-of-sale. So payments inherits §3.1's naming/discovery borrows and §3.2's relay-metadata discipline (an invoice fetch is small and timing-distinctive — `privacy-routing.md` gap 4 — so the relay-metadata minimization matters *more* for payments, not less).
- **Net:** for payments, iroh's lesson is *"build value transfer on top of a clean key-addressed transport + signed-log substrate — which iroh provides and we already have, but neither iroh nor we get the money part for free."*

| B11 (anti-pattern) | **iroh-docs' implicit/timestamp conflict resolution** | **Do NOT borrow.** Our `*-apply.cjs` reducers order by writer+seq / monotonic epoch, never `Date.now()` — keep it; it is the *stronger* model (`nostr-bridge.md` §7.2, `privacy-routing.md` §3.2) | All |

## 5. Honest note: where iroh is more mature vs where Holepunch is

**iroh is more mature *below* the data layer:**
- **Transport.** iroh's QUIC base (Quinn) gives standardized TLS 1.3, congestion control, loss recovery, stream priorities, and datagrams off the shelf; **QUIC Multipath** (all paths under one connection, per-path congestion control, seamless handoff) is a real, recently-landed 1.0 feature with **no Hyperswarm equivalent**. Moving **holepunch packets inside QUIC** (NAT-traversal draft) is a robustness win Pear's side-band UDX holepunch doesn't have.
- **Relay metadata posture.** Because iroh relays forward **ciphertext** and 90% of traffic leaves them, iroh's *default* metadata exposure is structurally lower than our **default-on, plaintext-terminating, content-pinning** HiveRelay. iroh is ahead on "private by default."
- **Discovery surface.** iroh ships **two** resolved transports (DNS-cacheable default + mainline-DHT opt-in + mDNS for LAN) behind one API; we ship the HyperDHT mutable-record path only. iroh's is broader and more battle-tested at the edges (DNS caching, offline LAN).
- **Stability signal.** iroh **core is 1.0** with an explicit stability commitment, independent perf tracking, and a clean "protocols vs platform" split.

**Holepunch / Pear is more mature *at and above* the data layer:**
- **Multi-writer.** **Autobase/Autobee** is a complete, shipping multi-writer linearizer with **explicit, author-written, clock-free deterministic conflict resolution.** iroh-docs is **pre-1.0 (v0.100.0), the least-committed iroh protocol, with under-specified conflict semantics**, and n0 signals its future is the still-**experimental Willow** (`iroh-willow`). On *principled* multi-writer convergence, **we are ahead.**
- **Signed ordered history as a first-class primitive.** Hypercore's **append-only signed log** gives replayable, attributable history by default. iroh splits bytes (immutable blobs) from index (docs) — powerful for dedup, but you **rebuild** ordered signed history yourself. Our catalogues/receipts/events *are* signed logs natively.
- **Companion-crate maturity.** iroh-blobs is **pre-production (v0.103.0 — "use 0.35 for production")**; iroh-gossip is **pre-1.0 (v0.101.0, no 1.0 date)**; iroh-docs **pre-1.0 (v0.100.0)**. Hypercore/Hyperdrive/Hyperbee/Autobase are production-deployed in shipping Holepunch apps (Keet) today.
- **A real app runtime.** Pear (Bare + Chromium) is a working application platform with an identity/key story (BIP-39 + per-app subkeys) richer than iroh's single-key model. iroh is **infrastructure, not a platform** — by its own "protocols, not platform" framing.

**Net:** borrow iroh's transport/discovery/relay *posture* (B1–B7); keep our data layer (B8–B11 borrow only the *algorithms*, never iroh-docs' conflict model). The two stacks are complementary, and our existing `relay-record.js` shows we already think in iroh-shaped primitives.

**Confidence caveats (carried from the briefing):** the iroh `concepts/relay` doc URL 404'd, so relay-metadata claims rest on the FAQ + `iroh-relay` README, not a dedicated concept page; and every Hypercore/Autobase/Nostr-vs-iroh contrast is *analytical synthesis* — the iroh side of each is cited, but n0 publishes no head-to-head comparisons, so the contrasts are reasoned, not quoted.
